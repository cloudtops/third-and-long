Game of the Week

Miami 28, New York 25

By Adam Long/Week 7

Miami Dolphins1-5
vs
New York Jets2-5
MetLife Stadium, East Rutherford, New Jersey

Yes, I’m just as surprised as you are. I didn’t think I was going to write one of these about either of these teams all season, and now we’ve got a good old fashioned two for one. When you think offensive explosions, surely you think about perennial Pro Bowl names like Malik Willis, Caleb Douglas, and Omar Cooper Jr. I am so happy for Dolphins fans that they no longer have to live in a world where their favorite team is winless. Although I’m not sure they’re so thrilled, as this is clearly a season for Miami where every win means a worse shot at the Jeremiah Smith sweepstakes. But to hell with tank talk, let’s talk Sunday’s victory. Fins Up!

Our heroes in teal and orange got off to a fast start, as QB Malik Willis marched his team down the field and scored on a quick strike to rookie WR Caleb Douglas. Dolphins’ RB Ollie Gordon II is an angry runner, and he has filled in very nicely for De’Von Achane in Miami’s backfield. He had a long, powerful run that moved the chains and helped start the Fins off strong.

It did not take long for the Jets to respond, though, as QB Geno Smith hit rookie WR Omar Cooper Jr. on a long ball that got New York into the red area. Smith fired a rocket to TE Mason Taylor a few plays later that had the score all knotted up at sevens. Three drives so far in this one, and three straight touchdowns to open the game, as Miami drove back down the field and scored on a 1-yard Gordon II touchdown dive to go up by a touchdown.

On the ensuing Jet possession, Smith made a poor decision to throw the football to the incorrect team and thus squandered solid New York field position. Luckily for Gang Green, Miami also squandered their opportunity and had to give the ball back to Smith. After a Jet punt, the Dolphins had the ball with a few minutes left in the half and an opportunity to steal a touchdown before the stoppage in play. Malik Willis put the entire state of Florida on his back on this drive, where he converted multiple fourth downs with his legs before powering his way into the end zone to put Miami up 21-7 at the half.

Two punts for both teams followed halftime, and a doink ended a Jets drive that actually had their offense moving the chains quite well. When they got the ball back, Cooper Jr. reminded everyone that he has arrived as a true WR1 in this league in his first year as a pro. Sorry Garrett Wilson, #11 is the best player in this receiver room, he has been on an absolute tear this year and shows no signs of slowing down. He had another huge play on this drive, an incredible concentration catch that set the Jets up nicely. Fast forward a few plays later, and Smith hit (Guess who!) Cooper Jr. in the flat to get the Jets back into the game with a touchdown.

After another Miami punt, Smith once again had his offense rolling, slinging the ball around the yard like vintage Favre. The Jets nearly scored on an RB Braelon Allen scamper that was ruled down at the one, but a penalty pushed them back to the five, and the Jets were unable to convert after Smith took a bad sack. New York tacked on a field goal to cut the Dolphin lead to 21-17.

Both offenses failed to score on their next drive, as multiple players fell victim to the architecture of MetLife Stadium. One of the main questions I would imagine architects ask themselves when building a football stadium is “Is the sun going to get in anyone’s eyes during the game?” But apparently I’m much smarter than most of these architects, as this is an issue that is much more annoying than you would initially think. Anyway, Miami got the ball back with five minutes remaining and a chance to ice the game with a touchdown and grab their first W of the season. What did Malik Willis do? He avoided a sack and threw a sick sideline pass to Douglas, who broke a tackle and sprinted down the sidelines to put the icing on the cake for the Fins. Douglas was dealing with an injury at the beginning of the year, but he looks as advertised and will certainly be a focal point for a Dolphin offense that has been largely unable to get it up so far this season.

But wait! It’s not over yet! Smith captained his offense back down the field and hit Taylor again on a 50-50 ball in the end zone that the big tight end was able to secure to keep the Jets’ hopes alive. After an extra shot at the two-point conversion, the Jets converted on a quick pass from Smith to… who else? Mason Taylor!

Fins’ ball with two minutes left. Gordon was rolling through the New York run defense, but was stopped short of the first down marker on a run that would’ve effectively ended the game. 4th and 1 now, and Miami HC Jeff Hafley had a decision to make. Punt the ball back to Geno with time to score, or trust your quarterback, who had been hooping all game, to get the win? Hafley chose the latter. Willis dove through the line on a sneak and won the game for Miami, their first green mark on the year.

Congrats Dolphins, and congrats to all the NFL fans that actually tuned into this one expecting a stinker and getting handsomely rewarded with a great football game. I can’t imagine I’ll be revisiting these teams at any point this year, so Dolphins/Jets fans, drink it up.
