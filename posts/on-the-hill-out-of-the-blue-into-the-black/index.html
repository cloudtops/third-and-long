Out of the Blue, Into the Black

By Henry Houghton/Week 8

8 games down, 4 to go. College football has once again blessed us with another action-packed, unpredictable, and drama-filled weekend of action. We saw three top-10 ranked teams fall this weekend (#2 BYU, #7 Ole Miss, #8 Texas Tech) which further blurs what our CFB Playoff picture will look like this postseason. While upsets are one of the most important aspects in all of college football, could the increase in upsets be a result of a more level playing field due to NIL and the transfer portal? With only 3 programs still undefeated (#1 Miami, #9 UCLA, #10 Utah), this marks the first time since 1977 that three or fewer teams remain undefeated through 8 weeks. If the last few weeks of games have proved anything, college football powerhouses are as volatile as ever. To quote one of my favorite Neil Young tunes, we are headed out of the blue and into the black.

In the midst of all the uncertainty this season, it seems there may only be one Power-4 conference that we can confidently predict who the conference champion will be… the ACC. Miami has been absolutely dominant so far this season and there doesn’t look to be a single team in the ACC who can hang with the Hurricanes. To be fair, Miami hasn’t really played anyone that would lead me to believe they are the BEST team in football, but they will get their first real test of the season next weekend, taking on #5 Notre Dame in South Bend. As crazy as this sounds, I don’t think a win or loss really matters for the Canes next week. With such a weak remaining schedule and a potential cakewalk to an ACC championship appearance, Miami could lose to ND and still be in a great position for a playoff berth.

The CFB Playoff committee once again changed their selection criteria this offseason to all Power-4 conference champions (SEC, Big Ten, Big 12, and ACC) receiving automatic bids to the playoff. And speaking of the Irish, the new selection format will offer a special exemption for one of the two independent teams left in the FBS (Notre Dame and UConn), guaranteeing a spot in the playoff if either team finishes in the top-12. Everyone and their mother wants the Irish to join a conference already. They constantly sit at the top of the polls, play cupcake opponents, and lose postseason games. Yes, I understand the Irish played a solid postseason in the 2024-25 season and made it to the national championship… but this is the only time in my lifetime that Notre Dame has shown up to play under the big lights. In 2020, they faced Alabama in the first round of the CFB Playoff only to lose 31-14. In 2018, they played Clemson in the first round of the CFB Playoff and got slaughtered 30-3. Saving the best for last, in 2012, Manti Te’o and the Irish faced Alabama in the national championship, losing 42-14. So to summarize, in the past 15 seasons, Notre Dame has made the playoff 4 times and got blown out in 3 of those games. Why do poll voters continue to take these guys seriously? The Irish haven’t won a championship since Lou Holtz was coaching. Rudy was released 33 years ago. I can appreciate the historical importance of the program and would like to see them back in contention… but just join a damn conference and earn your spot like everyone else!!!

Game of the Week

(#19) Texas 34 – (#7) Ole Miss 27

In what felt like a classic SEC matchup, the Texas Longhorns hosted the Ole Miss Rebels in a crucial in-conference battle on Saturday in Austin. Two highly coveted quarterbacks, two teams loaded with Sunday talent, and two squads with everything to lose. In what has become the trademarked phrase of the SEC and the easiest rage bait for any Big Ten fan: “It just means more” felt like the perfect sentiment for Saturday’s contest. The lights were bright, the fans screamed passionately, and just about everything in this game reeked of huge implications. The Longhorns have fallen well short of expectations this season, but Saturday could’ve marked a pivotal turning point in their postseason pursuit.

It was truly a tale of two halves at Darrell K Royal Stadium this weekend. Normally, that would entail one team coming back from a first-half deficit to win, but in this case, Ole Miss’ second half efforts came up just short. Texas QB Arch Manning clearly read and took offense to my article a couple weeks ago, where I called him “concerningly average”. Manning was surgical in the first half highlighted by a 50-yard bomb TD pass to Ryan Wingo and a rushing TD of his own, leading Texas to a 24-3 lead going into the break. It felt like Ole Miss never even got off the bus. Star QB Trinidad Chambliss has led an electrifying offense so far this season for the Rebels and has been in conversations as one of the best quarterbacks in the country. Unfortunately, Chambliss and the Rebs looked like a high school JV team in the first half. Maybe that’s just a testament to the strength of Texas’ defense, but this game felt like it was over even with 30 minutes of football left to play.

I’m not sure what Pete Golding told his squad at halftime, but damn did they respond! We weren’t even 2 minutes into the second half when the Rebels’ defense set the tone for the half with a huge strip sack on Manning which led to a fumble-recovery TD for Ole Miss. Finally, we had a game. Chambliss and the Rebel offense picked up on the vibe and completely changed their tune the rest of the game. Two quick touchdowns for Ole Miss followed and with 14 minutes left in the 4th quarter, we were tied at 24-24! Texas had lost all of their steam and completely passed the torch to Ole Miss. There was no way the Longhorns were going to choke away another lead, right? Texas just lost to Florida two weeks ago after starting off the game with a 17-3 lead. Surely, a team as good as Texas couldn’t choke two in-conference games in a 3-week span…

While Arch looked like a completely different player in the first half, his struggles became evident once again in the second half. Manning threw an absolutely horrible interception into triple-coverage with the game tied at 24. This throw was clip #1 in the ‘Quarterbacks for Dummies: What Not To Do’ training guide. Compared to his deep balls earlier in the game, I think Arch may have been a little concussed after taking a huge hit in the second half from an Ole Miss defender. It was that bad. Thankfully, the Texas defense had Manning’s back and finally held the Rebels to a punt after the interception. The Longhorns got the ball back with about 10 minutes remaining and the entire field to gain. Texas HC Steve Sarkisian finally remembered that his team can run the ball and RB Raleek Brown wasted no time when his number was called. Faced with a 2nd-and-9 at the Texas 21-yard line, Brown was given the handoff and exploded for a 47-yard run, putting Texas in Rebel territory. Two more running plays followed and backup RB Michael Terry busted out 31 more yards to put Texas 1st-and-Goal at the Ole Miss 1-yard line. The Longhorns capped off their impressive drive with an incredible play call, showing visions of Arch’s HOF uncle, Peyton. Bunched up under center in an I-formation, Arch dropped back to hand the ball off to Brown where the entire stadium expected a collision at the goal line. Instead, Arch had faked the hand-off on a play action QB keeper and trotted towards the end zone without a Rebel defender in sight.

Some late-game theatrics ensued but Ole Miss ended up coming just short of a historic comeback. Texas would hold on to win a thriller in Austin, 34-27. Both teams now have 2 losses, making it do-or-die for the rest of the SEC gauntlet. To make matters even worse for both squads, each team has an absolutely brutal schedule to finish out the season. I would be shocked if either team can find a way to finish 10-2. Texas will finish off their season with matchups against Mississippi State, #23 Mizzou, #15 LSU, Arkansas, and Texas A&M. Ole Miss’ path isn’t any easier as they will face Auburn, #3 Georgia, Oklahoma, and Mississippi State.
