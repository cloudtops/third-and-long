State of the Football

Week 10

By Adam Long

Here we are, we have arrived! The glorious midpoint(ish) of the 2026-27 NFL Football Season. How’s your favorite team doing? Good? Bad? Ready for next year? I hear you. How’s your fantasy football team doing? Terrible? Me too. Let’s jump into an action (and injury) packed week of ball.

A brutal Nor’easter swept through the upper right of our country this past weekend, and it started Thursday night in New Jersey. Pounding rain met the visiting Washington Commanders as they traveled to play against their hated rival, the New York Giants. Slow going early in this one until fireworks in the second half by Washington QB Jayden Daniels propelled the beleaguered Commanders to a much-needed victory. Despite two would-be game sealing interceptions by New York’s opportunistic defense (Daniels’ first interceptions thrown this season), Washington’s defense stood tall and gave Daniels and Company room to drive down the field in the waning minutes, where #5 fired a strike over the middle to WR Stefon Diggs for a game winning house call. The Commies improved to 3-5-1 and the glimmer of postseason hope remains… for New York, they stay atop the NFC East albeit with a slimmer margin, and the season outlook is still positive. Jayden Daniels has still never lost to the New York Giants.

To the biergartens of Munich we go! The Lions battled the Patriots in Germany Sunday, a one-sided affair where Lions’ RB Jahmyr Gibbs had his way with a New England defense that has been in a freefall as of late. Gibbs went for 250+ yards from scrimmage along with three total touchdowns, and became the first runner to eclipse the 1000-yard rushing mark this season (He ends this week with 1000 yards on the nose). The real story from this game, unfortunately, was that the Axis Injury Menace was alive and well, victimizing Patriots QB Drake Maye (ankle, likely done for the year), and Lions DE Aidan Hutchinson (ACL, certainly done for the year). Both of these clubs suffered major setbacks this week, although if the Lions offense can remain as explosive as they have this season they may still have a fighting chance.

Down in Louisiana, an NFC South matchup between the Saints and Panthers took place (whoopee!). Move over NFC East, this is the worst division in football. Carolina took sole possession of the top seed in the division by narrowly beating the Saints, improving to 4-5 after some last second heroics from their K Ryan Fitzgerald. The rest of the NFC South has roughly the same record, all at either 3-6 or 3-7. Tough sledding. This is Carolina’s division to lose, although the team has regressed somewhat following a slew of injuries to their offensive line and secondary. QB Bryce Young was serviceable but has yet to return to the peaks we saw from him during the first month or so of the season. New Orleans is getting fantastic production from their pass rushing unit (EDGE Carl Granderson and DE Chase Young are both top 4 in the league in sacks), but lack any ability to run the football on offense.

I don’t have a ton to say on the Dolphins and Colts game. Can’t imagine anyone faulting me for that. For Indianapolis, who got the win over Miami 24-22, that’s nice. 2-15 would’ve been rough. Especially considering you just lost all of your best players (not named Jonathan Taylor) and your WR1 is Laquon Treadwell. Miami is innocent in this loss specifically, as they had to roll with the young Syracuse product QB Kyle McCord, who was surprisingly competent during his first career start. Both of these teams will hold top 10 draft picks by season’s end. Something that impresses me about the Dolphins is how feisty this team can be at times, especially considering that eight of their starting 22 players are rookies (!).

The Browns and Texans met in I-Don’t-Believeland, where QB Deshaun Watson had me screaming at my television set all morning. I fucking hate that guy. I refuse to give credit where credit is due, although Watson has led a game winning drive for the Browns six times already this season, accounting for each of Cleveland’s six wins. Houston’s offense looked unstoppable until the second half, when Coach of the Year candidate Todd Monken whipped his Browns team into shape and outplayed the Texans soundly to keep pace in a tough AFC North division. For Houston, it’s a Murphy’s Law situation in 2026. This team is just not very good at winning games, and although their roster remains one of the best in football, the best teams in football actually know how to win games. Even more difficult for Texans’ fans to stomach is the fact that QB CJ Stroud is playing the best football of his career and would likely garner at least one MVP vote if the season ended today.

The savior has returned to Minnesota. I predicted big things from QB Kirk Cousins after the news that he had been traded back to the Vikings last week, and in one game back in the Purple and Gold, he has certainly delivered. Although his statline wasn’t eye-popping, the impact that serviceable quarterbacking has made to this football team is. Minnesota went into Lambeau Field and put belt to the rival Packers, absolutely dismantling one of the hottest teams in the league to the tune of 30-10. A league-average offense, which Cousins will likely deliver and then some, turns Minnesota from a question mark into a legitimate juggernaut capable of bringing the Twin Cities their first-ever Lombardi Trophy.

In a snoozer of an AFC South matchup, the Jaguars beat up on the lowly Titans by a final score of 40-23. HC Liam Coen has things rocking in Duval, with a record of 19-7 since taking over head coaching duties for the Jags. At 7-2, with the rest of the teams in their division holding either three or one win(s), Jacksonville has locked up the AFC South at the halfway mark. This team is really, really, really good. The defense is elite, and the additions they made at the deadline make that unit much better. The offense is explosive, and QB Trevor Lawrence is third in the league in touchdown passes and is also likely garnering some MVP consideration. Is it high time the Jaguars make their first Super Bowl appearance in franchise history? Time will tell, but I wouldn’t be the one to bet against it.

In another AFC stinker, the Bills beat up on the Jets on the road. QB Josh Allen was great in this one, and continues his absurd stretch on the ground. Allen currently has 11 rushing touchdowns this season, tied for the best number in football. Allen plays quarterback! Buffalo improves to 8-1 and like the Jaguars, has high hopes for their own first Super Bowl title. For the Jets, womp womp. This team is awful, especially considering the loss of QB Geno Smith who went down with an ankle injury. His status is to-be-determined, and rookie QB Cade Klubnik lowers the ceiling of this offense significantly. The Jets will finish the season as one of the league’s worst teams.

And yes, you guessed it, next up is another bad football game. Things have finally begun to make sense halfway through the year, and good teams are beating bad teams pretty consistently. The Chiefs are a good team, and beat up on the Falcons, who are a bad team. The arrival of WR Josh Downs gives QB Patrick Mahomes II a true gamebreaker, and breathes life into a team that has lost some steam down the stretch. The Falcons are no longer a mystery, as the play of QB Michael Penix Jr. has shown that he is decisively not the answer Atlanta’s brass thought he was when they selected him in the top 10 in 2024. Should’ve kept Kirko Chainz.

In our last instance of a terrible team getting blown out by a good (?) team, the Rams went down to Glendale and beat the Cardinals by four touchdowns. Despite a mess of injuries to Los Angeles on both sides of the ball, it appears as McVay finally has this team gelling when it counts. After starting the year out with a paltry 2-4 record, the Rams have quietly won four straight to keep their postseason hopes alive in a very difficult division. Things went from bad to worse in Arizona, where rookie QB Carson Beck was very bad against the Rams’ defense. Which is to be expected, of course, but I think Cardinals fans were expecting more from the mercurial former Heisman Trophy candidate. It appears as though Arizona will be spending their high first round pick on a quarterback in April.

A little further north in the Southwestern desert, the Seahawks beat the Raiders 21-13 on Sunday afternoon. Seattle QB Drew Lock made his fourth start and got his fourth win, a perfect record to go along with zero interceptions thrown in those games. It is time for a serious conversation, Seahawks fans. Lock simply gives your team the best chance to win right now, if for no other reason than he does not throw the ball to the other team (as Darnold has a penchant for doing), and he forces the ball to WR Jaxon Smith-Njigba. JSN is the best player in football, so this is a compliment. Seattle’s defense had rookie QB Fernando Mendoza in hell during the second half, where the Raiders offense gained -17 yards.

Finally, Sunday night put us to sleep with another one-sided affair between the Cincinnati Bengals and the Pittsburgh Steelers. Steelers QB Aaron Rodgers will have nightmares about this Bengals defense for the rest of the season, as he tied a career mark for longest streak without a completion (0/8) to start a game. The biggest story of the day was not the Cincinnati defense (who was incredible), but it was the offense. We can probably call the MVP race now. Bengals QB Joe Burrow has accounted for 4+ touchdowns in five straight games now, a mark that has not been reached since Peyton Manning did it in Indianapolis in 2004 (Manning rightfully won MVP that season). Burrow was essentially perfect in this game, and the gelling of the defense plus having their two star receivers healthy at the same time has vaulted Cincinnati to the top of my power rankings for the first time I can remember.

Monday night had everybody fired up and ready to go with one of the least palatable primetime games of the season. The 0-8 Chargers took on the 4-5 Ravens (who had dropped four straight following a stellar start to the year), and actually pulled out a win! This game was cruel and unusual punishment for anyone who had the misfortune of watching it. Fireworks were few and far between in this matchup of two of the league’s absolute worst teams, and me as a fan hated every second of it. QB Jacoby Brissett is not the answer in Baltimore, although he did throw for a season high 340 yards. Chargers QB Justin Herbert completed 12/18 passes for 129 yards, doing just enough to win. No gold medals from me for Mr. Herbert tonight, although I’m glad the Chargers are able to escape their nightmare 2026 adventure with at least one W.

Another week in the books, thanks for tagging along with me for this recap. I’m feeling like the Washington Commanders can do anything. We can beat anybody! Who we got next week?? Aaaaand fuck. It’s the Bengals.
