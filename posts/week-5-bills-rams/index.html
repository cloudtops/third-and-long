Game of the Week

Buffalo 37, Los Angeles 34

By Adam Long/Week 5

Buffalo Bills4-1
vs
Los Angeles Rams2-3
SoFi Stadium, Inglewood, California

11 total lead changes on Monday night in what was undoubtedly the most chaotic and exciting football game of the week, and probably the best game of the year so far. In a marquee matchup between two preseason Super Bowl favorites that have looked less surefire than we expected through four weeks of our young season, we the fans got our money’s worth and more from this one. This contest pitted our league’s last two MVP award winners against each other in Buffalo’s Josh Allen and LA’s Matthew Stafford, and both quarterbacks fully backed up their status as two of the game’s absolute best.

Let’s dive in. This one was riveting right from the opening kick, where Buffalo’s Ray Davis nearly cribbed it off the hop on a 70-yard kick return. Buffalo’s offense stalled in the red area and were not able to fully capitalize off the explosive, but K Tyler Bass (keep this legend in mind for later) tacked on three points for the Bills. The Rams’ K Harrison Mevis (keep this legend in mind for later, but for different reasons) missed his first field goal attempt on LA’s long opening drive, and Allen and the Bills drove down the field and again added another three points. Stafford responded with a short strike to WR Davante Adams for a touchdown, which was promptly answered by Buffalo in a 17-yard RB James Cook touchdown trot.

We went to the half with a six point Buffalo lead, a game that was tight but not as explosive as we may have predicted, and then all hell broke loose in the last 35 minutes. After an early RB Blake Corum touchdown dive for LA, back to back three and outs for the offenses opened the floodgates. Six straight touchdown drives ensued. It was answer after answer, with both quarterbacks putting their teams on their backs, a high-powered slugfest in which neither offense would be denied. After a late would-be game-winning touchdown run from Cook, Buffalo took the lead at 34-27, and booted the ball back to Stafford with a minute and a half remaining. The former first overall pick from 1958 drove his team down the field with authority, dissecting the Buffalo secondary with surgical precision on throw after throw until finally punching it into the end zone courtesy of RB Kyren Williams to tie the game at the end of regulation.

We go to extra innings! Buffalo received the opening kick, and wasted no time getting the ball to WR Khalil Shakir, who nearly broke free and scored on the first drive if not for what might’ve been a game-saving tackle by LA S Kamren Kinchens. On the very next play, Cook put the ball on the turf and the Rams recovered. Shades of Cam Skattebo. Please God don’t make me talk about that game anymore. Heartbreak for Bills fans, many of whom would not be blamed for switching off their television sets/laptops/phones at this point, as it was sudden death now, and Stafford was as hot as anyone in LA not named Jim Harbaugh. Stafford commanded his offense down the field, close enough to set up another Mevis field goal attempt to steal the win from the Bills. However, the stars aligned for Bills Mafia, and Defensive Player of the Year candidate Greg Rousseau got one of his big meaty paws on the football and blocked the kick! Vindication for Cook! Josh Allen promptly put the team on his back (literally, he gained 32 of the Bills’ 35 yards on that final drive with his legs), before Bass split the uprights from 45 yards out and won the game. An instant classic in LA, and Josh Allen’s first ever overtime victory. I did a double-take at that one too, don’t worry.
