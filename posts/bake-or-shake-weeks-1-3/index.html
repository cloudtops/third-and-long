Bake or Shake

The Gospel According to Baker Mayfield

By Nathanael Kjormoe/Weeks 1–3

Per the Accountable Sports Journalism: Guidelines for Covering Sports Responsibly, a sports journalist should:

  • Avoid conflicts of interest.
  • Establish a clear distinction between fact and personal opinion.
  • Promote positive sports values, fair play, and an environment of peace and understanding.

This op-ed… will be none of those things!

Here on Bake or Shake, we discuss one thing and one thing only: the greatness of Baker Fucking Mayfield.

If you’re looking for an unbiased, factual, straightforward piece of sports journalism, please continue looking. Because you’ve come to the wrong place.

Each week, I’ll take the time to analyze Baker Mayfield’s on-field performance and decide whether Baker did indeed BAKE, or whether he needs to SHAKE that performance off.

With Baker potentially returning in Week 7 against the Panthers, this seems like the perfect time to look back at his first three performances of the season before continuing through this glorious Baker run we’re all lucky enough to witness.

Buckle up. It hasn’t been the easiest start, but hey, greatness was never supposed to be easy.

Week 1: Cincinnati Bengals

Let’s begin with Baker’s season opener against the Cincinnati Bengals.

Heading into this game, Baker made some hefty statements. When asked about the upcoming season, he proclaimed, “I think we’re going to shock a lot of people this year,” continuing, “So I’m excited to just get on the road and do that.”

And oh boy, did he certainly surprise me…

Baker threw for a respectable 216 yards, completing 23 of 28 passes for an 82.1% completion rate (DAMN!). If that were his only stat line, we’d have ourselves a certified BAKE!

Unfortunately, Mr. Mayfield also took four sacks and lost three fumbles, including a particularly devastating one near the goal line that cost the Bucs a potential touchdown.

Now, was this Baker’s fault? Certainly fucking not. Well, maybe a little. But that’s beside the point.

Tampa Bay’s offensive line allowed a 38.9% pressure rate, surrendering four sacks, 11 total pressures, and 6 hurries. At this point, the offensive line was less of a protective unit and more of a welcoming committee for Cincinnati’s defensive front.

Now, PFF may have ranked Tampa’s offensive line third in the league heading out of Week 1, but anybody who watched Baker run for his life would know that’s a bullshit grade.

Despite his impressive accuracy, Baker couldn’t carry the entire team on his back, as he has so many times before. Tampa Bay fell to Cincinnati 33–27 on the road.

It pains me to say this, but three lost fumbles are difficult to defend, even for a man of Baker’s stature.

Week 1 Verdict: SHAKE

Shake it off, baby. We’ll get ’em next time.

Week 2: Cleveland Browns

Moving into Week 2, Baker had a lot of carrying to do, but still plenty of time to do it.

Our lord and savior led the Tampa Bay Buccaneers into battle against his former team, the Cleveland Browns. Surely this would be the week Baker reminded Cleveland of the greatness they so foolishly let walk out the door.

Well… not exactly.

Baker finished with 182 passing yards, completing 21 of 34 attempts for a 61.8% completion rate. He also threw one singular interception, but before you start pointing fingers at our beloved quarterback, let’s examine the evidence.

With 1:19 remaining in the first quarter, Baker knew his team needed a spark. Facing 1st-and-10, he targeted Ted Hurst III and delivered what I can only describe as a dart.

Unfortunately, Browns cornerback Myles Harden undercut the route, intercepted the pass, and returned it 25 yards.

Was Baker perhaps a little too confident in that throw? Maybe. Could Hurst have prevented the interception by not running such a lazy fucking route? I’d certainly like to think so.

Regardless, I refuse to let one interception define the performance of a former first-overall pick.

And Baker responded.

In the second half, Mayfield threw for 137 yards, one touchdown, and ZERO turnovers.

HOT DAMN! That’s Baking.

That’s the resilience we’ve come to expect from Baker Mayfield. Throw a pick? Who gives a shit? Get back out there and sling that football.

Unfortunately, Baker’s resilience couldn’t overcome his team’s shortcomings, and the Buccaneers suffered their second consecutive loss, falling 23–19 to the Browns.

The fucking Browns.

There were certainly flashes of greatness in this one, particularly in the second half. But with one touchdown, one interception, and another loss, I must once again deliver a verdict that brings me absolutely no joy.

Week 2 Verdict: SHAKE

Two weeks down. Two losses. Two Shakes.

I’m beginning to dislike this job.

Week 3: Minnesota Vikings

A concerning trend was beginning to emerge as the Tampa Bay Bucs entered Week 3 against the then-undefeated, and still undefeated, Minnesota Vikings.

Now, some may argue this was Baker’s worst performance of the season.

Personally? I saw a gutsy performance under nearly insurmountable odds against one of the league’s toughest defenses.

Baker threw for 217 yards, completing 17 of 34 passes, with one costly drop from our “star running back” Bucky Irving.

Yes, a 50% completion rate isn’t exactly something to celebrate. But before you start criticizing Baker’s accuracy, I’d encourage you to examine the circumstances.

Baker was under duress all night, taking SIX sacks for 31 yards lost.

Six. Fucking. Sacks.

Remember that concerning trend I mentioned earlier?

It’s Tampa Bay’s offensive line, which apparently believes protecting its franchise quarterback is more of a suggestion than a responsibility.

Through three games, the Buccaneers had allowed a staggering 13 sacks, averaging 4.33 per game and ranking DEAD FUCKING LAST in the NFL.

It’s a near-impossible task to ask any quarterback to perform under that much pressure, even if that quarterback happens to be first-overall pick Baker Mayfield.

But the most inexcusable moment came with 1:54 remaining in the fourth quarter.

Facing 4th-and-13 and trailing 20–16, Minnesota’s pass rush forced Baker to scramble backward nearly 20 yards.

(Lol, classic Baker.)

Rather than surrender to the inevitable sack, Baker did what Baker does best.

He played fuckin ball.

Attempting a desperation throw to keep his team’s hopes alive, Baker’s throwing hand collided with Vikings linebacker Blake Cashman, dislocating his right thumb.

The heart displayed on that play was nothing short of impressive. Was scrambling backward 20 yards the smartest decision? Probably not. Was it the most Baker Mayfield thing imaginable? Absolutely.

Unfortunately, the resulting injury was considerably less inspiring.

Tampa Bay fell to Minnesota 23–16, dropping to 0–3 and leaving Baker with an injured throwing hand.

Three games. Thirteen sacks. Three losses. And now our franchise quarterback is hurt.

At this point, I don’t know whether to blame the offensive line, the football gods, or my own hubris.

While Baker showed incredible tenacity and grit, three straight losses and a dislocated thumb are certainly not baking.

Week 3 Verdict: for the love of god, FUCKING SHAKE IT OFF.

And somebody PLEASE get this man some protection.

Three Weeks In: Is There Hope?

Now, I know what you’re thinking.

Three games. Three losses. Three Shake verdicts.

What the hell happened to the greatness of Baker Fucking Mayfield?

Well, my friends, I’m glad you asked.

Despite Tampa Bay’s miserable start, there are some legitimate positives that absolutely should not be overlooked.

In Week 1, Baker reminded everyone just how accurate an elite quarterback can be, completing an astonishing 82.1% of his passes.

Against Cleveland, he demonstrated the resilience that makes him so damn entertaining. Following an early interception, Baker responded with 137 second-half passing yards, a touchdown, and zero additional turnovers.

And let’s not forget the legs.

While Baker isn’t your traditional running quarterback, we all love watching that beautiful bastard scramble downfield. Through three games, he contributed 65 rushing yards and a rushing touchdown against Cincinnati.

Perhaps most importantly, Baker has continued to keep his team competitive despite circumstances that would have lesser quarterbacks questioning their life choices.

All three losses came by a single score.

Six points against Cincinnati. Four against Cleveland. Seven against Minnesota.

That’s not a team getting blown off the field. That’s a team that’s painfully close to figuring things out.

And who better to lead that turnaround than Baker Mayfield?

Final Thoughts

Look, I’m not going to sit here and pretend Baker has been perfect.

He hasn’t.

There are turnovers to clean up, throws he’d like back, and decisions that even I, a devoted member of the Church of Baker Mayfield, have a difficult time defending.

And trust me, my devotion runs deep. I have a literal prayer candle with Baker Mayfield’s face on it. At this point, I might have to light that bad boy and say a few Hail Mayfields in hopes of turning this season around.

But what I refuse to do is ignore the grit, resilience, and determination this man has displayed while playing behind an offensive line that apparently has a personal vendetta against him.

Baker Mayfield is a competitor. He’s a leader. And most importantly, he’s the kind of quarterback who makes football worth watching.

If Tampa Bay can figure out how to protect its quarterback for more than three seconds, and his receivers can start creating some separation, there’s no reason this season can’t turn around.

Whether Baker returns in Week 7 against Carolina or Week 8 against Atlanta, one thing is certain:

I’ll be watching. I’ll be defending. And I’ll be ready to witness the glorious return of Baker Fucking Mayfield.

Three weeks. Three Shakes.

But the oven’s just getting warmed up, baby. It’s time to Bake!

Ya boi,
Nathanael Alan Kjormoe.
