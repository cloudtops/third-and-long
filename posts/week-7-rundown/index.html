Week 7 Rundown

By Adam Long/Week 7

The time is 10:33PM, the date is October 25th, 2026. We have made it to the good old days, my friends. We are now fully entrenched, elbow-deep into the real meat of the NFL season, and I for one could not be happier. Most teams in the league now squarely fit into one of three boxes. We have the real contenders, the teams that I and most others predict will be able to make strong runs into January. We have the Who Knows? clubs, the teams who could either make it into the dance via a wild card spot or finish the season losing ten straight. Finally, we have the cannon fodder, the teams who purely exist so the contenders can make their resumes look just a touch better come playoff time.

I am in a delightful mood this evening as I sit down to pen this particular recap, as this week just so happened to be one of my favorites of the year. It was the Commanders’ Bye Week, of course! I was very glad to not have to subject myself to the self-flagellation that comes with the title of Diehard Commanders Fan, if only for one Sunday. So without further ado, let’s talk ball.

A real clash of the titans in Chicago kicked off the week, with the Bears emerging victorious over the visiting Patriots. If you’d like to take a gander at my full take on Thursday’s action, go here. Sunday started bright and early in the beautiful city of Paris, France, where QB Aaron Rodgers led his Steelers to victory over the freefalling New Orleans Saints. This was the first NFL game ever to be played in France, and the timing could not possibly have been any better. Paris is just beautiful this time of year. Church facades burning in the sunrise, bird songs accompanied by the wailing of sirens and gunshots. Lovely stuff. The Steelers finally got a strong showing from their expensive defense, holding Shough and the Saints to a measly ten points. Rodgers and RB Jaylen Warren did just enough to secure the W and improve to a surprising 5-2 mark on the year.

The 49ers went down to Georgia, not for a fiddling contest, but to play the Dirty Birds in Atlanta. Two teams on completely different trajectories in this one coming into Sunday. The Falcons were coming off a tough loss to the Bears in which they were outmatched in every phase, and held a 2-4 record. The Niners were coming off a close win against the Commanders, but had won six straight and held one of the last remaining undefeated records. Pretty easy one to pick, right? Wrong. San Francisco was unable to get anything going against the Falcons, who were propelled to victory by way of their stout defense and a strong showing from QB Michael Penix Jr. and WR Drake London. The substation has claimed numerous big names in San Francisco so far this season, and the injuries finally proved too much for the 49ers to overcome.

In Cincinnati, the battle of Skyline Chili vs. Chesapeake Bay Crabcakes went down, a proxy war in the age-old clash between Bengal and Raven. Baltimore QB Lamar Jackson did not play yet again, and Cincinnati rolled to a three score victory due to the strength of their defense and a return to form for QB Joe Burrow and their offense. They scored all five of their touchdowns in the second half, a blazing hot finish for one of the most balanced teams in the league. Baltimore did not have any answers for Cincinnati’s passing attack, and QB Snoop Huntley was unable to move the ball effectively in the red area against a Bengal defense that has been better than expected this year. Huntley did set a new career high mark for passing yards, but it was too little, too late.

Down to the Carolinas we go, where the upstart Panthers hosted the cellar-dwelling Tampa Bay Buccaneers. Carolina QB Bryce Young has been one of the best signal callers in the league so far this season, but he was absolutely suffocated by the Bucs’ defense, and the Panthers were decimated utterly and completely at home. A 30-point loss at home to a division rival with one win is not a good look. Tampa Bay did return QB Baker Mayfield in this contest, who had missed the last few games with a thumb injury, but it was the ferocity of this defense and the Buccaneer running game that left Charlotte burning. RB Bucky Irving continues to excel, he has 300+ rushing yards and three scores in his last three games. This Tampa Bay team could still be sneaky down the stretch in a very confounding division.

The New York Football Giants traveled south to Houston, where RB Cam Skattebo figured out how to hold onto the football with both hands in overtime. The red-hot Texans could not handle the Giants’ rushing attack, and the three-headed monster of Skattebo, Tracy, and Singletary propelled NYG to an upset victory. New York actually looks relatively competent under the guidance of QB JJ McCarthy. Eighteen kind of looks elite? So don’t take my word for anything, I guess. This Giants front seven is honestly probably good enough to win a Super Bowl, and the rest of the team is rounding out nicely as well as the season goes on. This is what I was afraid of when Harbaugh came to town. Super Bowl? No, probably not. NFC East winner? Sigh.. probably.

In the Twin Cities, the Colts and Vikings squared off in one of the most boring games I have ever had the displeasure of watching. The Vikings offense still sucks, and RB Aaron Jones aggravated a knee injury that will sideline him for at least the next few weeks. Luckily for Minnesota, this is one of the most insane defenses I have ever seen, and can absolutely bring the Vikings their first ever Lombardi Trophy if the offense is even league-average. I know you can’t give out MVP awards to coaches, but defensive coordinator Brian Flores would get my vote in a world where they gave me one. For Indianapolis, take solace in the sacking of GM Chris Ballard, because right now that’s pretty much all you’ve got. Minnesota is now the sole threat to the 1972 Miami Dolphins with the only undefeated record left in the league at 6-0, and the Colts are in hell.

Take a look at my recap of my Game of the Week here. Yep. I know. Dolphins/Jets was the best one this week? Yes. It was awesome. Shut up.

On to the witching hour! Or I guess just the late slate. Can Scott Hanson come up with a cooler name for the late window games? Seems like there’s a lot of meat left on that bone for him. Browns vs. Titans was honestly pretty exciting, and Cam Ward actually threw for more than 75 yards! 181 today. The kid is heating up! He has only thrown for 200+ passing yards once this season in seven games. Yikes. The Browns got a victory on a field goal as time expired, propelled by a breakout game from rookie WR KC Concepcion. Cleveland possesses an embarrassment of riches in young talent on both sides of the football, as Concepcion and fellow rookie WR Denzel Boston both look incredible in year one. The Browns are now 5-2, and it’s no longer a fluke. Their defense is genuinely elite, led by a very strong core of guys on rookie deals. If QB Deshaun Watson can just not suck complete ass, this team can be a perennial contender. Big ask, I know.

Denver vs. Arizona was surprising to me for a number of reasons, chief among them being Bronco QB Bo Nix learned how to play football again. A 62-yard TD sprint from Nix highlighted his big day, a day where Denver scored 30 points and Sean Payton finally got the offense rocking again. I was less surprised by the final score, as Arizona is certifiably Not Good and I think Denver is certifiably Good. Not much to say on the Cardinal side, outside of the fact that I think it’s cool when a tight end leads the league in receptions, so shout out to Arizona TE Trey McBride. And shout out to all the tight ends out there, Happy National Tight Ends Day!

A very interesting game in Detroit, where the Packers lost to the Lions on a last second field goal. I’m not going to say a peep about the officiating in this one. Not one single word. The fact that NFL referees feel like they need to prove themselves to the nth degree this year after labor disputes in the offseason is not only destroying the public’s faith in the integrity of the game, but also making a lot of professional football borderline unwatchable. I’m all for labor disputes, but holy shit, the zebras need to relax. The amount of costly penalties is hurting the product. It’s something every fan knows, and this year is the worst it’s ever been. Add in the broad legalization and commercialization of sports gambling taking center stage on every single commercial break, and it is getting genuinely difficult to refute theories that Vegas is actually calling. Roger Goodell, fix these officials. THEY ARE RUINING THE GAME. Anyway, Green Bay got robbed due to two of the stupidest roughing calls I have ever seen and Detroit improved to 4-2.

The Los Angeles Rams rolled over the Las Vegas Raiders in a game that would’ve effectively ended the Rams’ season if they had lost. I am sad about the Raiders, it seems like a lifetime ago that they were 3-0 and vibes in the desert were high. They are now 3-4, and after getting blown out by the Rams today and the Patriots a few weeks ago, it might be time to see what you have in first overall pick QB Fernando Mendoza? Kirko Chainz is still playing well, but with the rapid regression of the defense, there’s really not a lot of options on the table for Vegas, specifically with the Broncos and Chiefs both excelling within their division. Rams, good job I guess? You beat up on a bad team like you were supposed to. Do you think you’re special or something? Try beating a real team, like the Cardinals.

Finally, Sunday night had the Kansas City Chiefs flying to the PNW to take on the defending champs. The Chiefs entered this contest at 5-0 riding the hot hand (or foot, I guess) of RB Kenneth Walker III, the Seahawks entered on a two game losing skid due to costly turnovers from their QB Sam Darnold. In short, the Chiefs were completely outmatched in every single aspect of the game of football in this one. This Seahawks team looked identical to the team that blew everyone out in the playoffs last year. Darnold played effectively (outside of the end zone pick), JSN is JSN (JBest SPlayer in the NLeague), and this defense is the best in the league. I would give all of my money to DraftKings if it meant getting to watch Mike Macdonald’s defense against Brian Flores’ defense in the NFC championship this year. That would be so awesome. That would probably end like 9-3 or something, and I would love every minute of it.

Despite looking relatively competent offensively early in the Monday night matchup, the Philadelphia Eagles have thrown in the towel. Seems like GM Howie Roseman finally missed when he paid DT Jalen Carter and DT Jordan Davis a combined $230 million, as this whole defense is checked out. Props to Dallas for getting the primetime win, I guess. Dak looked pretty good, and Dallas was able to run the ball extremely well (200+ yards!) on a team that has no fire. I fully expect a reckoning in Philly, and soon. The Sirianni experiment worked out great for you, Eagles fans, but now is the time to turn over a new leaf.

Another week of ball in the books. Thanks again for following along, we’ll see you next week, same time, same place.
