2026 NFL Trade Deadline

By Adam Long/Week 10

Well, it’s everyone’s favorite time of the year again! The midpoint of the NFL season, the trade deadline. Endless possibilities awaited us this week, as all 32 teams were subject to flipping completely upside down with the introduction of a new superstar, or a franchise cornerstone hitting the road. Well, not actually. And especially not this year. This was one of the sleepiest deadlines in recent memory, particularly when looking backwards on last season’s fireworks. Instead of going through each team and determining who was a seller and who was a buyer, I’ll give you my ten picks for the most impactful trades that happened either on Tuesday or in the week or so preceding. Without further ado, let’s jump in.

1. Kirk Cousins, QB. Raiders to Vikings

Undoubtedly the most impactful move of this year’s deadline, Tuesday saw Vegas shipping off their starting quarterback to his former home. This was an absolute home run for both teams. The Raiders only had to pay Cousins $650,000, and all they got in return was nine weeks of excellent quarterbacking, half a season of mentorship to their heir-apparent rookie QB Fernando Mendoza, and terrific offense week in and week out. Minnesota got a competent player to lead their offense, which has been nothing short of pathetic so far this season. No one is more excited about Cousins’ return to the Twin Cities than WR Justin Jefferson, who began his record-setting career with Kirko under center, and has regressed pretty significantly over the last stretch due to the Vikings’ QB carousel. Cousins has been excellent this season and considering how good this defense is, immediately turns Minnesota into a true Super Bowl contender.

2. Jeffery Simmons, DT. Titans to Bears

In what would’ve been the biggest home run of the deadline if the Cousins move didn’t go down, the Bears pushed all their chips to the middle of the table in the acquisition of all-world defensive stalwart Jeffery Simmons from Tennessee. This is a win-win in every aspect, Chicago gets one of the best players in football to improve their front seven and make a real run at a Super Bowl, and Tennessee gets more chances to hit in the draft. Simmons was traded for TWO (!) first-round selections, a price tag warranted by his elite play. He has already been excellent in two games with Chicago, and will assist the Bears deep into the playoffs this season and in years beyond.

3. JJ McCarthy, QB. Vikings to Giants

Huh? Nine? Number three on this list? Yep, I know, I’m as surprised as you are. Nine (who shall furthermore be referred to as Eighteen), has been excellent since getting traded to the Big Apple a few weeks ago in relief of Jameis Winston. The Giants will need to have a serious conversation in the offseason if McCarthy can continue his excellent play through the season in New York. The Giants are on a collision course with the playoffs, and a lot of their hot start can be attributed to the success Eighteen has had through the air targeting WR Malik Nabers and TE Isaiah Likely. For Minnesota, no harm, no foul here. A draft pick is better than having McCarthy in your locker room specifically.

4. Jessie Bates III, S. Falcons to Jaguars

Although relatively quiet this season outside of one jaw-dropping interception a few weeks back, Bates remains one of the league’s best defensive backs. The three-time All Pro safety makes the move to Jacksonville, who were big time buyers at the deadline and appear to be making a serious Super Bowl push themselves. The Jaguars have the fifth-best rushing defense in football, and bolstering their secondary with a veteran superstar like Bates will only help move this franchise towards their first Lombardi. Atlanta, one of the most confounding teams so far this season, get a decent grade on this one, as they sent Bates down I-95 for a second and a fifth round selection.

5. Josh Downs, WR. Colts to Chiefs

Sweet relief for Chiefs Kingdom. After the injury to WR Tyquan Thornton, it appeared as though Kansas City was set to throw away another year of Hall of Fame QB Pat Mahomes II’s prime. Instead, Brett Veach took a swing on one of the league’s more underrated wide receivers in the acquisition of Indianapolis speedster Josh Downs. Downs immediately establishes himself as a WR #1 for Mahomes, and will help keep this offense two-dimensional as they look to return to the top of the mountain. I don’t love this trade for the Colts, as they shipped off one of their best young players amidst their exodus of aging stars.

6. Quenton Nelson, OL. Colts to Seahawks

Continuing with the Colts, Indy sent future Hall of Fame guard Quenton Nelson to the Pacific Northwest in exchange for a third and a fifth round pick. This one is a fleece any way you slice it. The Seahawks get one of the league’s best offensive linemen for peanuts, and although Nelson is on the wrong side of 30, he will likely retain that designation for at least the next half decade. Nelson immediately steps in as a top-tier starter for an offense with enough firepower to make another run, but one that just needs to establish some semblance of consistency. Nelson is exactly that stabilizing force for Seattle, and will provide excellent mentorship to the Seahawks’ young-yet-talented offensive line.

7. Joey Porter Jr., CB. Steelers to Cowboys

The Cowboys took another swing on a premier defender ahead of this year’s trade deadline, acquiring one of the league’s most underrated cornerbacks in Joey Porter Jr. This is a fantastic trade for Jerry Jones (I’ve never written that before), as Porter immediately steps in as a starter for one of the most beleaguered units in football. Back injury notwithstanding, JPJ will greatly help improve a sinking defense in the run fit and in the secondary. In four games with Dallas, Porter has allowed two receptions for ten yards on nine targets. Too bad they can’t clone him to help out with the other half of the field, where CB DaRon Bland has been barbecue chicken this year.

8. Jacoby Brissett, QB. Cardinals to Ravens

The Ravens do not appear to be waving the white flag just yet. The acquisition of Brissett provides a stabilizing force to an offense in desperate need, an offense who has trotted out rookie QB Joe Fagnano for most of the last two games to disastrous results. Baltimore is in a free-fall, but Brissett is competent enough to get this team a handful of wins in a tough division. All he really needs to do is get the ball to WR Zay Flowers and the Ravens should have a pretty good shot to get things back on track. For Arizona, hindsight is 20/20. Immediately after finalizing the move that sent Jacoby to Maryland, QB Gardner Minshew II injured his elbow and was placed on season-ending IR. It is now the Carson Beck show in Glendale.

9. DeForest Buckner, DT. Colts to 49ers

Hey, it’s the Colts again! Sensing a theme here? Indianapolis sent Buckner back to San Francisco in exchange for a fourth round pick. I’m pretty happy with this one for both sides, as Indy sent an aging (but effective) run stopping tackle with top-end speed to a non-conference opponent for draft capital. The Niners get back one of their own, who will help steer this defense to championship contention. Buckner is still a very good player, and provides necessary depth for a team more impacted by injury over the last few seasons than any other.

10. Jordan Davis, DT. Eagles to Jaguars

Among other defensive linemen the Jaguars acquired before the deadline, Davis is certainly the headliner. Although experiencing a bit of a down year (along with the rest of the Philadelphia defense), Davis is one of the most fearsome run-stuffers among all defensive interiors. A behemoth of a human being, Davis stands at 6’6” and weighs in at just over 330 lbs. He will further raise the ceiling for one of the best defenses in football. For Philadelphia, I don’t want to be too quick to pan Howie Roseman for trading away a great young player, as I know that Howie is probably the best GM in the business. I was expecting more of a fire sale though, as Philadelphia is in desperate need of new faces at most positions in order to help the dumpster fire that is 2026.

So there you go! Again, not a ton of fireworks this year, but some very impactful deals nonetheless. Who do we think will make the biggest impact down the line for his new team? My vote is Cousins, but I could see any of these 10 players making a huge difference as the season winds down.
