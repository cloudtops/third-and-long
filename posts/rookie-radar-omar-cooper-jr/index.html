Rookie Radar

Omar Cooper Jr., WR, New York Jets

By Noah Labao/Week 6

“Touchdown! Wow! Omar Cooper! Unbelievable!”

Gus Johnson, Indiana @ Penn State

Four weeks on injured reserve with a high-ankle sprain had buried the Jets’ first-round investment before it ever got going. Then Omar Cooper Jr. came back and reminded the Gang Green exactly why they traded up into the first round to get him. Against the Patriots he led the Jets with six catches for 103 yards and the first touchdown of his career, a 48-yard strike from Geno Smith in the third quarter that briefly dragged a flagging offense back into the game. It wasn’t enough in a 26-19 road loss, but it made him just the third Jet since the merger to post a 100-yard receiving game within his first two career outings, joining Garrett Wilson and Derrick Gaffney, and earned him a Pepsi Rookie of the Week nomination.

College Bio: Indiana Hoosiers (2022 to 2025)

Cooper saved his best for the brightest stage. After two quiet early seasons, he broke out as a deep threat in 2024 (21.2 yards per catch) and then became a volume star in 2025, leading the receiving corps on Indiana’s first-ever national championship team with 69 catches for 937 yards and 13 touchdowns. He left Bloomington among the most productive pass-catchers in program history, fourth on the Hoosiers’ career receiving-touchdown list with 22, and a favorite target of Heisman winner Fernando Mendoza down the stretch of the title run.

High School Recruiting Profile: Lawrence North HS (Indianapolis, IN)

A hometown product, Cooper starred at Lawrence North in Indianapolis as a three-sport athlete (football, basketball, and the long jump) before committing to stay in-state at Indiana. A four-star recruit by some services, he piled up 132 catches for 2,856 yards and 22 touchdowns across his prep career, with a senior line of 43 grabs for 702 yards and seven scores to go with a well-rounded basketball stat sheet. The multi-sport athleticism shows up in his game today.

The measurables back up the tape: a compact 6-foot, 199 pounds with 4.42 speed and a 37-inch vertical, good for a 9.16 Relative Athletic Score.

Scheme Fit: Why He Works in New York

The Jets’ offense needed a receiver who could win now, and Cooper slotted in as exactly that in his first extended look. He’s a polished route runner with reliable hands and the deep speed to stretch a defense, versatile enough to line up outside or in the slot; he was a vertical outside threat early at Indiana before thriving from the slot in his breakout year. Paired with Geno Smith, who’s on a record pace, Cooper gives the Jets a chain-mover who can also take the top off, and the 48-yarder against New England was a glimpse of the ceiling. Health is the only real question after the ankle; the role is there for the taking.

Similarity Comp: Rashee Rice

The closest template is Rashee Rice: a sturdy, do-it-all receiver who wins with physicality, polished routes, and run-after-catch juice rather than elite size. Like Rice, Cooper is comfortable working the slot or the boundary, plays bigger than his frame, and turns short and intermediate targets into chunk plays. If he stays healthy and the target share grows, a Rice-style high-volume, chain-moving role is a reasonable projection for what he becomes in New York.
