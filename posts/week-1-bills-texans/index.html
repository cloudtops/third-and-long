Game of the Week

Buffalo 36, Houston 31

By Adam Long/Week 1

Buffalo Bills
vs
Houston Texans

After a heartbreaking loss in the divisional round last year, the Buffalo Bills start off 2026 with a chip on their shoulder and eyes for their franchise’s first Lombardi Trophy. Storylines galore ahead of this one on whether or not QB Josh Allen could get it done against a Texans defense that has given him fits in recent years. We got a fairly resounding “Yes” in that regard, as the Bills’ signal caller, who accounted for 357 total yards to go along with four total touchdowns, led Buffalo to an electrifying win over one of the league’s most vaunted defenses. After dealing with the lack of a true blue #1 target for Allen over the last few years, the Bills may have finally found their man in newcomer WR D.J. Moore, who came over from Chicago. Moore accounted for 100 yards on the nose to go along with a touchdown grab, and cemented himself as Buffalo’s top receiving option alongside TE Dalton Kincaid. Kincaid, who excelled against the Texans’ staunch secondary in his own right, hauled in five passes to the tune of 130 yards. Allen has made a strong case for his second MVP season through one week of play. Buffalo’s defense looked lost at times, but eventually played just well enough to secure the W against Houston QB C.J. Stroud, who showed shades of his excellent 2023 form in which he won Offensive Rookie of the Year. Stroud guided the Houston offense right down to the wire until their last-gasp opportunity was foiled by Stroud’s second lost fumble of the contest. Turnovers have plagued Stroud in recent years, as the quarterback’s pair of fumbles (including the one that lost the game) surely had Texans fans flashing back in horror to the absolute disasterclass that was their 2025 divisional round matchup in New England. Houston’s biggest bright spot on offense looks to be the addition of RB David Montgomery, who accounted for 79 total yards and three total touchdowns.

The biggest surprise for me in this matchup was how outmatched Houston’s defense looked against Allen and the Bills. Allen cemented himself and his squad as the apex of the AFC, and Houston now has a longer road ahead, especially if their calling card may not be as vicious as advertised.
