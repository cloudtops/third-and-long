Rookie Radar

Emmett Johnson, RB, Kansas City Chiefs

By Noah Labao/Week 8

Emmett Johnson turned one carry into 15 yards and caught all five of his targets for 128 more, including a 73-yard touchdown from Patrick Mahomes, the first score of his career against Bo Nix and Co. His 143 scrimmage yards were the third-most by any rookie this season. Kansas City lost 28-24, but Mahomes said afterward that the offense is going to need more of what Johnson showed, and the Chiefs faithful will surely agree.

College Bio: Nebraska Cornhuskers (2022 to 2025)

Johnson’s final season in Lincoln was the best by a Husker back in a decade. He ran for a Big Ten-leading 1,451 yards and 12 touchdowns in 2025, the program’s first 1,000-yard rusher since 2018 and its biggest total since Ameer Abdullah in 2014. He was named the Big Ten’s running back of the year. He also caught 46 passes for 370 yards, which pushed him to 1,821 all-purpose yards, second among running backs nationally. That receiving work is exactly what showed up against Denver.

High School Recruiting Profile: Academy of Holy Angels (Richfield, MN)

Minnesota isn’t a running back factory, which may explain why Johnson slipped through. As a senior at Holy Angels he carried 326 times for 2,484 yards and 42 touchdowns, numbers that would have drawn far more attention in Texas or Florida. Nebraska took him, and he spent three seasons earning his way to a starring role before his senior breakout.

He measured 5-foot-10 and about 202 pounds at the combine with a 4.56 forty. He isn’t a burner, but his 77th-percentile hands for the position tell you where his value lies.

Scheme Fit: Why He Works in Kansas City

Andy Reid has always found a role for a running back who can catch, and Johnson fits the mold. His one carry in Week 8 matters less than his five targets, because the Chiefs used him as a receiver out of the backfield and he made Denver pay for leaving him uncovered. With Mahomes looking for someone he trusts underneath, Johnson’s routes and hands give him a path to steady snaps even if he never becomes the lead runner. Offensive coordinator Eric Bieniemy has already talked publicly about getting him more opportunities.

Similarity Comp: James White

Akin to Super Bowl 51 hero James White, Johnson was a Day 3 back out of the Big Ten who made his name catching passes rather than piling up carries, and White went on to become one of Tom Brady’s most trusted targets on third down. Like White, Johnson isn’t built to carry the ball 20 times a game, but he runs good routes, catches cleanly and turns short passes into first downs. White’s best moments came in big games, and if Johnson keeps earning Mahomes’s trust, he could have the same kind of role.
