State of the Football

Week 8

By Adam Long

Alright boys and girls, how was that one for ya? Week 8 has probably been the best week of football yet in my eyes. Is that because my Commanders got a big win and the Cowboys blew a double-digit fourth quarter lead to lose to the (checks notes) lmao, Cardinals? Yeah, probably. But we had some real classics this week! Let’s dive in, match by match, as I guide you through all of the fireworks from Sunday.

Well, technically we’re not starting with Sunday, but with Thursday! A real marquee matchup between legit MVP candidates Jordan Love and Bryce Young took place on Thursday night in chilly Green Bay. The visiting Panthers were coming off an embarrassing division loss to the Buccaneers, and the Packers were coming off of Roughing the Passer-gate. Both teams were pissed off and needed a victory to get things back on track. Green Bay lit up the reeling Carolina secondary and ended up securing the win not only with the efficiency of their passing attack, but the stoutness of their pass rush, keeping Young’s options limited through the air.

Off to JerryWorld, where the Cowboys looked to be heating up. After a thorough drubbing of the Eagles in primetime last Monday, the “Greatest Offense There Is” was looking past the visiting Cardinals. A classic trap game for Dallas, although trap games generally come when the better team loses. Dallas is just not a very good team period. This defense is (say it with me!) the worst in the NFL. I don’t want to take anything away from Quarterbacking Icon and Absolute Legend QB Gardner Minshew, who lit up the Dallas secondary all day and led his Cardinals to a much-needed win. This Arizona team is scrappy, man, and Minshew still looks great slinging the pill. I expect the Arizona Football Cardinals to notch a few more wins under their belt this season as they will undoubtedly look to play spoiler to the bigger fish in their division.

Oof, Jacksonville vs. Indy is our next one. Take a look at the final score of this one. Yep, there’s your analysis. Nothing to be said here aside from the Jaguars are a top-end NFL team and the Colts are a very bottom-end NFL team. Add in that Indy’s morale has likely never been lower following the exodus of future Hall of Famer OG Quenton Nelson to Seattle and the departure of future Hall of Very Good-er DE DeForest Buckner back to California, and you have a recipe for a 42-7 loss. Jonathan Taylor and Josh Downs will be repping new teams here before the end of this week, I would be surprised if the Colts won another game this season. For Duuuuuval, nice work beating up on a weak division opponent. Lawrence and Tuten look great, and we even had a Brian Thomas Jr. sighting! Probably just looking to boost his value before the deadline. Indianapolis appears on a collision course with Jeremiah Smith, and Jacksonville has all but wrapped up their division after eight weeks.

The Browns and the Steelers rematched after Week 4’s Thursday Night matchup, and the Steelers ended up getting the last laugh in this one after losing to their division rivals in primetime. This time, a full beatdown to the tune of 27-6 resulted, where TJ Watt and the Pittsburgh pass rush had QB Deshaun Watson (rightfully) in hell all day. Watson did throw for 300+ yards with help from TE Harold Fannin Jr., who got loose for 154 yards and has quietly been one of the league’s best receiving tight ends this season. The Steelers take over position A atop the AFC North and rise to 6-2.

In one of the strangest football games that you will ever see, the Tampa Bay Buccaneers defeated their division rival Atlanta Falcons in a Florida monsoon. Two different offensive philosophies going head to head here. The Falcons, who have a great running back, would not stop trying to throw the ball. The Buccaneers, who have several great running backs, did not stop running the ball. Falcons QB Michael Penix Jr. completed roughly 30% of his passes and threw four interceptions. Buccaneers QB Baker Mayfield did not complete a pass on three attempts. Tampa Bay dominated 16-0 behind nearly 300 rushing yards from their backfield. Both NFC South clubs are now 3-5, and the division is wide open.

Ravens and Bills was an unfortunate one if you love football, as Buffalo got a 36-10 victory in a very boring game. The story from this one was not Buffalo’s dominance but rather the fact that Baltimore QB Lamar Jackson was injured on his very first play after returning from an ankle injury that had sidelined him for nearly a month. Jesse Minter announced that they had placed Jackson on injured reserve after the game, effectively ending Baltimore’s already injury-muddled season. For Buffalo, the NFL’s best team took care of business against the reeling Ravens, and Josh Allen is quietly on pace to break Cam Newton’s QB rushing touchdown record through eight weeks. He needs six more to do it, and has ten games remaining.

Turning now to a much better football game, the Detroit Lions handed the red-hot Minnesota Vikings their first loss in nearly one calendar year. Minnesota was riding an eleven game win streak into this contest, not having lost a game since the addition of defensive coordinator Brian Flores last season. This Vikings defense is absolutely the best in football, and held the high-flying Lion offense to only 19 points in this one. Unfortunately, Minnesota’s offense is abysmal. When looking at offensive EPA statistics, the Vikings rank higher than only the Titans and the Chargers. Very poor company, to say the least. WR Justin Jefferson had an unbelievable touchdown catch, but there is very little hope for a deep postseason run in the Twin Cities unless HC Kevin O’Connell can get his offense to start scoring routinely. Especially considering the meat grinder that is the NFC North.

A shootout in the Big Apple! Just like we all expected. The Raiders bounced back from a four game losing streak to take out the Jets on the road, propelled to victory behind big days from QB Kirk Cousins and TE Brock Bowers. Cousins has quietly been one of the league’s most efficient passers, and looked fantastic against a very, very, very bad Jet defense. On the bright side for Gang Green, the offense is actually pretty good! QB Geno Smith, one of the last true gunslingers in football, is putting up points, and the Jets have a very balanced, explosive offense. Weird, right? On that note, it is time for HC Aaron Glenn to be shown the door. Can’t have a defensive-minded HC running a team with such a poor defensive unit.

If you’d like to check out my thoughts on the Game of the Week between the Titans and Bengals, click here. (Yes, you’re reading that right. I’m tired of writing about Chiefs games. I know they lost, but it’s been years.)

The Battle of SoFi took place last Sunday between the Chargers and Rams. Booooooring. News flash! Justin Herbert has completely forgotten how to play quarterback. The Chargers are the worst team in professional football and the way they are playing right now, 0-17 is not out of the question. The Rams also don’t look very good but at least were able to climb back to 4-4 following a very poor start. That keeps them in the thick of things in the NFC West (sort of), but any further stumbling from the preseason darlings will effectively end their season. And who comes to town next week, but the big, bad Washington Commanders…

The Patriots and Dolphins played in a divisional clash, where Mike Vrabel and his squad took care of business against an inferior opponent to get right back on track and improved to 5-3. Although the score was close (26-19), I felt the Patriots were pretty firmly in control from wire to wire in this one. These teams know each other better than anyone, and divisional games halfway through the season can get weird. New England got the win here based on their coaching staff and team just being better than Miami’s. Hard-hitting analysis, I know, but what? You want me to talk about how exciting it is watching Rhamondre Stevenson run between the tackles? Get real.

In what probably should’ve been the game of the week, the Broncos defeated the Chiefs to improve to 6-2. The Chiefs fell to 5-2 and have now lost two straight after starting the year off undefeated. Denver has seemed to assuage most of their offensive concerns in recent weeks, as HC Sean Payton has once again gotten comfortable calling plays and QB Bo Nix has started to turn it on. This defense can propel Denver to a deep postseason run yet again. Kansas City, trade for Josh Downs. Or sign Tyreek Hill. Or just find literally anyone who can play receiver.

And now, for the moment you’ve all been waiting for! THE WASHINGTON COMMANDERS WON THIS WEEK!!! Jump around, sing, dance, kiss a baby. Party like it’s 2024! Now I know that we beat a Philadelphia Eagles team that has spent more time in the emergency room than in the end zone this season, but damn it, a divisional win is a divisional win, especially with all the pain Philly has put us through in recent years. After jumping out to a ten point lead in the first half, the Eagles hid under the bed for the rest of the game as QB Jayden Daniels reminded everyone that he’s still really good. Despite Washington’s procedural woes this year, Daniels has been extremely effective when he has been on the field. Beat the Rams on the road next week and anything is possible in this division. Yes, I’m clearly biased. No, I do not care. Write your own damn article!

Monday night was one of my most anticipated games of the year, the immovable object vs. the unstoppable force. Caleb Williams and his Chicago Bears came to town to take on the defending champs, and put the entire league on notice. Chicago dominated the vaunted Seattle defense, winning in convincing fashion to the tune of 34-17. Chicago is now firmly my midseason favorite to win the NFC and advance to their first Super Bowl in 20 years. Seattle is currently being held hostage from success this year by offensive coordinator Brian Fleury, their first year man who still somehow does not understand that the Seahawks should target WR Jaxon Smith-Njigba 95 times per game if they want to win. He is leading the NFL by 259 receiving yards, and is currently on pace to break every single major receiving record in a season. Seattle still has a chance to make it to the playoffs with their elite defense, but they need to play lights out with how good the rest of the conference is.

So there it is folks, another week, another dollar. Or whatever it is. Tune in next week when I write 3500 words about how the Commanders went across the country and dominated the Rams and won by 50 points. Calling my shot!
