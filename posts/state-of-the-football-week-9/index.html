State of the Football

Week 9

By Adam Long

Another week, another stinging disappointment for my Washington Commanders. I believe I am nearing the end of my rope. I hope you all forgive me if I have less to say or seem less chipper than usual, cheering for this pathetic excuse of a team is making me actively hate football. So, if your club is doing well, or got a win this weekend, good for you! Must be nice. If your team, like mine, also sucks, then perhaps you will take solace in commiseration. Let’s dive into all of the games from this past weekend.

Thursday night was kicked off with a real headlining contest between the Super Bowl-hopeful Jaguars and the reeling Ravens. Due to injuries to both (!) of their quarterbacks, Baltimore was forced to start rookie signal caller Joe Fagnano. He played poorly, which was to be expected, but the 33-10 loss is not on his shoulders. Injuries have certainly held this team back from contention, but you can’t blame a four-game losing skid entirely on the injury report. Minter needs to find an identity for his team and fast, as the AFC North is slipping further and further away every week. The addition of QB Jacoby Brissett should likely buoy the ship and keep Minter’s first-year seat cool, even if the losses continue to pile up.

The NFL made its annual pilgrimage to Madrid this past Sunday, as the Spanish city played host to the red-hot Cincinnati Bengals and the very forgettable Atlanta Falcons. Cincy QB Joe Burrow continued his MVP campaign, throwing for three more touchdown passes to extend his lead over the rest of the league. Cincinnati’s receiving duo of Higgins & Chase continued to excel, as both receivers scored and Ja’Marr got loose for 10 grabs and 155 yards. For Atlanta, this team is still a mystery to me. I’m pretty confidently able to say that they are not good, but how bad they are is hard to discern. QB Michael Penix Jr. again looks to be regressing, but if RB Bijan Robinson can continue his OPOTY candidacy this team always has a chance to steal a W.

In Louisiana, QB Shedeur Sanders took over the Browns’ offense from Deshaun Watson who missed Week 9 due to concussion protocol. (Lol). Despite Sanders not turning the ball over and actually playing pretty serviceably in his stead, the Saints were able to get the victory to finally earn a much needed win and stay afloat in the NFC South. QB Tyler Shough, who has cooled off significantly following a very impressive start to the year, was able to find TE Juwan Johnson on a flag route in the end zone to put NOLA ahead in overtime. Although Sanders drove his team down the field (TE Harold Fannin Jr. scored on one of the best individual efforts of the year), Cleveland was unable to convert on what would’ve been the game-winning 2-pt conversion try. To quote JuJu Smith-Schuster, “The Browns is the Browns.”

The “football game”, if you could even call it that, between the Chiefs and the Jets went down Sunday at Arrowhead. Chief QB Patrick Mahomes II bounced back from a rough showing against the defending champs last week in resounding fashion, even fighting through what looked like a really gnarly elbow injury. KC was able to get their bellcow, RB Kenneth Walker III, back on track, who had a very nice showing himself to the tune of 100+ yards and three (!) touchdowns from scrimmage. The Chiefs should remain optimistic about their postseason chances in a division that looks cuddlier than initially advertised.

In Charlotte, Bryce Young and his Carolina Panthers played host to the visiting Denver Broncos in a rematch of Super Bowl 50 (which I found out to be the only Super Bowl not to be notated with Roman numerals, as Super Bowl L would’ve looked strange in marketing materials. That’s our research difference!) Denver looked firmly in control until Young stormed back, bringing Carolina within one score late in the game before falling short. Bryce bounced back from a few less than stellar weeks with a respectable offensive output in this one, especially considering his patchwork offensive line. On the flipside, not a lot for you here, Denver faithful. Good teams get wins, no matter how it looks. The Broncos are a legitimate contender.

Despite my poor mood on this Miserable Monday, something that did brighten my spirits this past weekend was the continued implosion of the Dallas Cowboys. Now I know the Cowboys got a win over the vaunted Indianapolis Colts (lol), but man, being a Cowboys fan might be the worst football fate imaginable. Every year you’ve got hope, big dreams of returning to the glory of the ’90s. And every year, the Football Gods crush all of that good will in increasingly hilarious fashion. Dallas is better than their 4-5 record on paper, but football is played on grass (or turf, because Jerry is too much of a stingy bastard to care about player safety). Barely eking out a win against a cellar-dwelling Colts team that has entirely moved on to 2027 is funny, we can all admit it!

Not sure what I did to deserve having to watch the Eagles/Giants game, but man, that was a rough few hours. The Eagles only escape the dreaded #32 spot in my power rankings because the Chargers exist. That’s very pleasing for me to be able to say. Similarly to the Ravens, the Eagles have been completely decimated by injuries, but this does not completely absolve HC Nick Sirianni of all wrongdoing. The product on the field would be untenable in any market, but when you think about Philadelphia’s fanbase, I’m surprised Sirianni is still alive. 2-15 is not out of the question for the Birds. For the Giants, this team will almost certainly win their division and make the playoffs, if for no other reason than their competition being terrible. This is a well-coached team with a really nasty defense, and that generally bodes well for teams as we wade into these colder winter waters.

I guess there was a game in the nation’s capital on Sunday, but does anyone actually want me to talk about that? I sure as hell don’t. Can we just skip it? Here’s my analysis: the Commanders are a bad football team. An average-at-best roster held hostage by terrible coaching week in and week out. The Rams are an average football team, held together by the power of their superstars (see WR Puka Nacua and DE Myles Garrett) and not much else. They will likely miss the playoffs as well. Next!

The Detroit Lions beat the Miami Dolphins to improve to 6-2, behind a stellar performance from their much-maligned defense. This defense has absolutely flipped a switch in the last month to become one of the league’s best units after the worst-case-scenario they had going on in September. RB Jahmyr Gibbs is an OPOTY frontrunner, and QB Jared Goff continues to fly under the radar as one of the most underrated and effective players in the NFL. DE Aidan Hutchinson is on a tear, and leads the entire league in pressures through nine weeks (50). Whichever team can emerge from the gauntlet that is the NFC North will be the Super Bowl favorite, and I think right now it’s a four-way coin flip. Well, maybe a three-way coin flip. Sorry, Vikings.

A really fun day for Texans fans. This defense played like they should, and QB CJ Stroud built on a dark horse MVP argument. Five touchdowns and 400+ passing yards for their captain, who picked apart the floundering Los Angeles defense with ruthless efficiency. Stroud simply had all the answers for Chris O’Leary’s test on Sunday. Houston improves to 3-5, not out of the hunt yet by any means, especially if this defense can continue to gel. DE Will Anderson Jr. leads the league in sacks, and is also my midseason pick for Defensive Player of the Year. Chargers fans, I won’t kick you while you’re down. Nothing else to say about this team that I haven’t said already. 0-8 is 0-8.

The Dark Side reminded the league that they were still among the best defenses in football, keeping Arizona QB Gardner Minshew and the Cardinal offense in hell all Sunday. The Seahawks were on cruise control, delivering a decisive victory for their rabid fanbase and keeping hopes alive in the Emerald City. Hawks fans would likely appreciate more points than 17, but considering that they started a backup quarterback and still won by double digits, that’s probably comfortable enough. I still think this division is up in the air, it just depends on what version of this team we get for the rest of the year. When they’re on, they’re borderline unbeatable and look like one of the dominant teams we’ve seen in the last decade or so. But they’re not that team all the time, so it’s up to HC Mike Macdonald to get his squad to start stringing together some W’s down the stretch.

Staying with the NFC West, the San Francisco 49ers punched the Raiders in the face off the jump, and did not take their foot off the gas all day. Although Vegas had TE Brock Bowers proving he is the best tight end in the league (Sorry Trey McBride), this one was all Niners from wire to wire. QB Brock Purdy continues to excel off-schedule, and RB Christian McCaffrey is flying under the radar relative to his output. San Francisco is certainly still a contender, even if they feel less dominant than they did before the loss of WR Mike Evans.

A lot of points scored in the contest between the Packers and Patriots. Big day for the over. Green Bay rolled to victory due to another offensive explosion from QB Jordan Love, and the opportunistic Packer defense intercepting New England QB Drake Maye thrice in the first half. This team looks so good when they are playing up to their full potential, I think all football fans would agree with me that there are few sights as beautiful to behold on the gridiron than Love letting one rip deep. And he sure does do that a lot. Why wouldn’t he? He has one of the best pass-catching tandems in the league with WR Christian Watson and WR Matthew Golden, who are third and fourth in total receiving yards this year respectively. Imagine if they could catch!

For Sunday Night Football, we go to your local high school cafeteria. A bona fide food fight in this one, where Tampa Bay QB Baker Mayfield and Chicago QB Caleb Williams combined for an eye-popping six interceptions. Williams threw four picks himself, and was genuinely terrible after a very effective first quarter. Luckily for Mr. Williams, the Bears are a very good and very dangerous football team. Well coached teams will beat poorly coached teams more often than not, and Tampa HC Todd Bowles has been over his skis all season. Despite two interceptions (including a pick-six that essentially won the game for Chicago), I’ll maintain Mayfield’s innocence in this one. It’s hard to throw a football with a still-kind-of-broken right thumb. Chicago improves to 7-2, and Tampa Bay falls to 3-6, tied for the most wins in their division.

Finally, a Monday Night brawl between the league’s best offense (Buffalo) and the league’s best defense (Minnesota) in Minneapolis. A snoozefest through and through, as QB Josh Allen and the Bills were finally able to break through the Vikings’ steel curtain in the second half, powered by three turnovers from embattled Minnesota QB Carson Wentz. Simply put, Wentz had one of the most baffling games possible, with bad decision after bad decision. He could not stop throwing the ball to the defense. Minnesota’s defense performed admirably against the league’s top scoring unit, but the loss was ultimately attributable to the complete lack of assistance from the Viking offense. Minnesota now has two straight losses after winning six straight to open the season, and now starts an intra-divisional gauntlet. Buffalo cements their status at the very apex of the National Football League, coming in at #1 in my power rankings for a third straight week.

Alright everyone, thanks for bearing with me through another tough week. Hopefully next week we get a little more excitement from the slate, as this one has honestly been a bit of a sleepy one. I just looked at the Week 10 schedule… and it doesn’t look great.
