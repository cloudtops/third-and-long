Rookie Radar

KC Concepcion, WR, Cleveland Browns

By Noah Labao/Week 7

“Did I stutter?”

KC Concepcion

Denzel Boston had been getting the highlight clips. In Nashville, Concepcion got the targets, racking up a total of ten, and Tennessee spent the afternoon regretting each one. A career-best 129 yards, his first triple-digit game in the NFL. Three carries for 23 more, the longest an 18-yard burst in the fourth quarter that set up a Browns touchdown. Cleveland won 27-24 on a walk-off, stayed alone atop the AFC North at 5-2, and the rookie came away with a Pepsi Rookie of the Week nomination and a place among PFF’s 15 highest-graded first-year players. The Browns keep on rolling, as another wideout on that Dawg Pound offense finally emerges to help lighten the load for Deshaun.

College Bio: NC State (2023 to 2024), Texas A&M (2025)

Unsurprisingly, NC State found out quickly not to blink around KC. Concepcion’s true freshman season in 2023 rewrote the Wolfpack’s freshman record book: 71 receptions, 839 yards, 10 touchdowns, plus 320 yards on the ground for good measure. The ACC named him Rookie of the Year and Offensive Rookie of the Year, and the FWAA put him on its Freshman All-America team. Year two was leaner (53 catches, 460 yards, six scores), so he packed for College Station. Texas A&M got the best version of him. In 2025 he caught 61 balls for 919 yards, topped the SEC with nine receiving touchdowns, brought two punts back for scores on 456 return yards, and finished fourth in the league with 1,450 all-purpose yards. Three seasons, 185 catches, 2,218 yards, 25 receiving touchdowns.

High School Recruiting Profile: Julius L. Chambers HS (Charlotte, NC)

The story starts in Rochester, New York, and picks up speed in Charlotte, where his family moved when he was three. At Chambers he collected two state rings, the 2019 and 2020 North Carolina 4AA titles, and as a junior posted 36 catches, 458 yards, eight receiving scores, and three punt-return touchdowns. Scouting on Concepcion was drastic and oftentimes inconsistent between mainstream ranking organizations. Rivals saw a four-star and the No. 8 player in the state. Other services had him as a three-star buried past No. 500 nationally. NC State took the chance, and one season later the debate was over.

Size isn’t the selling point at 6-foot, 190 pounds. The first two steps are. College scouts kept coming back to his release off the line as one of the quickest in the class.

Scheme Fit: Why He Works in Cleveland

As of right now, the Browns are blessed to have a WR room that schematically shouldn’t be there – a productive multi-role wide receiver corps. Boston takes the top off, while Concepcion lives underneath, where a crossing route can turn into a footrace. Tennessee saw both halves of the plan on Sunday, as Cleveland called concept after concept over the middle and let his acceleration handle the rest. The handoffs matter too. His college coaches used him as a runner and returner, the Browns have started doing the same, and that gives a run-heavy offense one more way to get the ball to its best athletes. Through seven games he has 30 catches for 315 yards, in a role that keeps getting bigger.

Similarity Comp: Percy Harvin

In 2009, Minnesota took Percy Harvin 22nd overall; seventeen years later, Cleveland grabbed Concepcion two picks later. The overlap doesn’t stop at the draft board, as Harvin came out at 5-foot-11 and roughly 185 pounds, a 4.41 burner who did his damage as a receiver, a ball carrier, and a return man, and he parlayed that resume into Offensive Rookie of the Year. Concepcion leans more receiver than runner (Harvin carried it nearly 200 times at Florida), yet the job description reads the same. Get him the ball in space. Hand it to him once or twice. Make the first defender guess. Cleveland’s Week 7 game plan looked like it was drawn from Harvin’s old Vikings tape.
