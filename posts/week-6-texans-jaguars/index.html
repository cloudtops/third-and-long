Game of the Week

Houston 34, Jacksonville 31

By Adam Long/Week 6

Houston Texans2-4
vs
Jacksonville Jaguars4-2
Wembley Stadium, London, England

I’d like for you to imagine for a moment, a foggy autumn morning in London. As you sit sipping your tea, nestled in a comfy chair in your favorite café overlooking the River Thames, you notice a crowd of people in strange kits meandering along past your perch. This rowdy group is clad in sports jerseys, unfamiliar sports jerseys to you, a lifelong Londoner. Your closet is full of Arsenal shirts, and the gaudy dark teal duds in front of you offend your dainty European eyes. These people are diehard fans of the Jacksonville Jaguars, a team just over 4,200 miles (sorry, 6,800 kilometers, you dorks) away from London, a club who plays a sport light years away from the football that you know and love.

It is very interesting to me, as someone who has never really looked at the NFL’s commitment to scheduling more and more international games every year as a net positive for our game, that the people of London have really taken to their surrogate home team. The Jaguars now hold their British residency for two weeks a year across the pond, and this year is the first year I can remember when international games haven’t been, for lack of a better word, shit. The decision for the NFL to hold important divisional games thousands of miles away from both teams’ home stadiums seems absurd, but it may just turn out to be another Goodell Gamble that delights NFL owners and disenfranchises NFL fans.

Anyway, you’re probably here to hear about the game, not to listen to me think out loud about the future of our league and what schedules may look like in a decade (I can’t wait for the Rio de Janeiro Royals to take on the Berlin Tigers before their Week 29 bye). So let’s talk about the game, god damn it. Don’t let both teams’ records coming into this game fool you, these are two good football teams. The 1-4 Houston Texans came into this game as 2.5 point underdogs, although their rival Jaguars held a 4-1 mark through five weeks. The Jaguars have looked like one of the league’s most complete teams of the young season, and the Texans have looked competitive albeit not able to deal finishing blows to get W’s. Both teams entered Sunday coming off wins last week, the Jaguars getting their fourth in a nail biter vs. Philadelphia, and Houston getting their first win of the season in a thorough drubbing of the lowly Titans.

A bit of a snoozer early in this one, the only touchdown of the first quarter came on a short strike from Texans QB CJ Stroud to TE Foster Moreau. Jacksonville QB Trevor Lawrence threw an interception early in the second quarter, but rebounded nicely with two touchdown passes to RB Bhayshul Tuten. Stroud and the Texans answered the bell, adding a K Ka’imi Fairbairn field goal and Stroud’s second scoring pass of the contest, again to Moreau. Houston headed to their locker room leading by three.

Things grew considerably more exciting in the second half. The Texans offense controlled the ball down the field, capping off a strong drive with a Stroud-to-WR Jaylin Noel touchdown. Two punts ensued, and then the Jaguars roared back. Lawrence hit a pop pass to rookie TE Nate Boerkircher, who climbed the ladder and secured his first professional touchdown catch with one hand, a highlight-reel play for the young man. Stroud again retorted by commanding his Texan offense to paydirt, courtesy of a 25-yard scamper from RB Jawhar Jordan.

Enter Travis Hunter. 2025’s second overall pick and former Heisman Trophy winner has shown flashes of greatness in his young career, but Sunday was truly his coming out party. Arguably the most impactful two-way player since Green Bay’s B.J. Raji, Hunter has clearly learned a lot from his former head coach and mentor Deion Sanders. As soon as Jacksonville got the ball back following the Jordan touchdown, the Travis Hunter show started. He had three receptions on that drive, one explosive catch and run for 26 yards, a 12-yard catch that moved the chains, and a 7-yard grab for a Jaguar touchdown. Fast forward a few minutes later, and Stroud had driven his team down the field yet again and was knocking on the door of the end zone with a chance to go up 10. Stroud rolled to his right and floated a pass to WR Xavier Hutchinson, but Hunter jumped the route to secure his second career interception. For all of you living under a rock for the last five or so years, Travis Hunter excels at both wide receiver and cornerback.

The Jags once again produced on offense, converting on a 60-yard field goal try from arguably the best placekicker in the league, K Cam Little. I haven’t talked a lot about Stroud in particular during this recap, and that’s because someone else can do it better than I can. He was sensational today, and has been sensational all season. Despite the Win-Loss record for the Texans, Stroud is, at least in my eyes, in the thick of the MVP race. He shook off the late pick and rebounded by steering the Houston ship to victory by way of a 49-yard Fairbairn field goal as time expired.

What a game! Really woke me up this morning. The AFC South is a difficult egg to crack so far this year, if Jacksonville had won this morning and resigned Houston to 1-5 while themselves climbing to 5-1, they probably would’ve had this division locked up. Now, especially with the Texans actually looking like we thought they would in the preseason, who knows? The winner of this division was absolutely playing in this game today. Which team is it? Flip a coin.
