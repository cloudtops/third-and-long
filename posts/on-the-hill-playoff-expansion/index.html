On the Hill

Playoff Expansion

By Henry Houghton/Week 7

What a week in college football! Five of the AP’s top-7 teams fell this weekend, marking an unprecedented day of upsets since the poll was introduced in 1936. Back in 2014, we saw five of the top-8 teams go down in a single weekend, but never have this many top-ranked teams fallen in a single day. If the weekend proved anything, it’s that college football is as unpredictable as ever… and with the College Football Playoff Committee announcing plans on October 15th to expand the playoff from 12 teams to 24, the sport may be entering its most transformative era yet.

Playoff Expansion

When is enough going to be enough? According to multiple sources, CFP leadership and ESPN officials have reached an agreement to expand the playoff field from 12 to 24 teams beginning in the 2027 season. This is bad news for anybody who likes good things. Sure, I can understand the argument for fans getting to watch more football… but what does this mean for regular-season games? Power 5 teams will have no reason to schedule big in-conference games (other than mandatory opponents) and even less of a reason to schedule strong out-of-conference opponents. If you grind out a few wins against mid-tier in-conference opponents and play a bunch of cupcake out-of-conference games to fill in the gaps, good chance you’ll be sitting in the top 4-5 range of your conference and have a great chance to make the playoff.

In theory, I like the idea of more mid-major programs earning a spot in the playoff and trying their luck against the big dogs. But more often than not, these teams are getting spanked once they face a blue-blood program. We saw a strong James Madison program put up a solid fight in the 2025 CFB Playoff against Oregon, but adding more of these games seems more like a cash-grab and a participation trophy for some of these schools, rather than a legit opportunity to win it all.

Just like everything else in the world, it all comes down to money. ESPN will continue to make billions of dollars off of the expanded format and schools will profit from more opportunities to play football games. It is what it is. As a fan, it seems crazy that I’m complaining about more football games being played… but there is something special about midseason, in-conference games that ultimately decide your postseason fate. These are the games that the fans look forward to all season and leave the longest legacy. I hate the idea of an 8-4 SEC team who is ranked 24th in the country making the playoff. Like yeah, they are probably one of the best 24 teams in the country and have played an incredibly hard schedule, but did they really earn the opportunity to play for a national championship? Football does not need to turn into March Madness. I was more than happy with a 4-team playoff and think most collegiate programs probably were too.

One of the most important uncertainties that the expansion threatens is conference championship games. Last year, the CFB Playoff committee told us that teams shouldn’t be punished for earning a spot and then losing a conference championship game. So if losing the game doesn’t matter, what is the point of playing it? Sure, bragging rights and potentially better seeding. But aren’t the #3 and #4 ranked teams in the conference getting the better end of the deal because they don’t have to play an extra game, risk injury/fatigue, and waste their time on a game that has minimal playoff implications? If you’re ranked inside the top-20 teams, I would think you’d be a lock for the playoff. Realistically, the SEC and the Big 10 will both have 4-5 teams each who will make the playoff. I’m really not sure how playing in a conference championship is a “reward” for the best teams in each conference anymore.

Game of the Week: (#3) Ohio State 19 – (#6) Indiana 15

Our last two national champions and top-dogs of the Big 10 squared off on Saturday in what was one of the most highly anticipated matchups of the 2026-27 season. Anytime Gus Johnson is on the call, you know it’s gonna be wild. In what started as a grid-locked, defensive battle, the scoring heated up a bit at the end of the game for an insane finish. Heading into the 4th quarter, Ohio State was up 13-7 and it was still clearly anyone’s game. Superstar WR Jeremiah Smith had been relatively quiet all day and was due for a big play at any moment. Smith’s time came just 2-minutes into the 4th quarter when Julian Sayin tossed up a 38-yard moon ball towards the endzone where Smith boxed out two defenders and came down with the TD.

Just when Ohio State thought they had some momentum, Indiana came flying back the following drive and punched in a quick TD followed with a 2-point conversion and cut the lead to 19-15. On Ohio State’s next drive, Buckeye fans’ hearts stopped when Sayin was hit in mid-throwing motion and came down hard on his shoulder. Sayin went straight to the locker room and was replaced by true freshman Luke Fahey. Both teams traded possessions after a couple three-and-outs and finally, the Hoosiers had the ball, down 4, and a chance to win the game. Indiana QB Josh Hoover led an impressive drive down to the Buckeyes’ 27-yard line where they faced a 4th-and-4 with the game on the line. Hoover took the snap in shotgun and launched what looked like a beautiful toss to the back left corner of the endzone to WR Nick Marsh. Marsh had the defender beat by at least 2 steps but the open space failed to be enough as Marsh caught the ball with his left foot out-of-bounds. Indiana failed to score the go-ahead touchdown and Ohio State went on to win a thriller in Bloomington.

For the defending national champions and coach Curt Cignetti, this marks their first loss in 666 days (number coincidence? I think not!) and 22-straight games. Even for a Big 10 team, what an incredible streak of dominant football. Cignetti and the Hoosiers have a relatively safe schedule for the remainder of the season and still have very strong odds to reach the college football playoff and Big 10 championship. As far as the Ohio State Buckeyes, they got revenge from last year’s Big 10 championship loss, but at what cost? Coach Ryan Day confirmed that Sayin would be out for at least “several weeks” which puts a big question mark on the Buckeyes’ future as they get ready to face USC, Oregon, Northwestern, Nebraska, and Michigan.
