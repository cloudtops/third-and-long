Rookie Radar

Arvell Reese, LB, New York Giants

By Noah Labao/Week 9

Big Blue’s No. 5 overall pick was unblockable all afternoon, finishing with 12 tackles (10 solo), three sacks, three tackles for loss, three quarterback hits and a forced fumble. The strip-sack was the play everyone will remember. Philadelphia had just recovered a muffed punt, and Reese gave the ball right back. New York sacked Joe Milton seven times in a 19-11 win, and Reese was the main reason the Eagles had no way to slow down the pass rush. He’s just the fifth Giant ever to record three sacks against the Eagles in Philadelphia.

College Bio: Ohio State Buckeyes (2023 to 2025)

Reese’s college career started slowly, plagued by a combo of injuries and drowning via positional depth. A concussion limited him to six games as a freshman in 2023, and he didn’t record a stat. He was part of the 2024 national championship team, then broke out completely in 2025 with 69 tackles and 6.5 sacks. That season made him a consensus All-American and the Big Ten Linebacker of the Year. His academics improved just as much: he went from a 0.4 GPA early in high school to a 3.7 in college and was a two-time Ohio State Scholar-Athlete.

High School Recruiting Profile: Glenville HS (Cleveland, OH)

Glenville is one of the best pipelines to Ohio State, and Reese was the 24th player legendary coach Ted Ginn Sr. has sent to Columbus since 2002. He was a four-star recruit, the 18th-ranked linebacker in the 2023 class and one of the top five prospects in Ohio. He won a state championship, playing on a defense that didn’t allow a point in six games that title season, and was named a Division IV Defensive Player of the Year.

At 6-foot-4 and 243 pounds, he has the size of an edge rusher and the range of an off-ball linebacker, and that combination is what makes him so hard to plan for.

Scheme Fit: Why He Works in New York

Most rookie linebackers have one job. As a Harbaugh rookie though, your given title is only the beginning, as John and staff rotate him between inside linebacker and edge rusher depending on the matchup. That flexibility has been there from the start. He led the NFL in solo tackles through four weeks and won Rookie of the Week in Week 4. Against Philadelphia, the Giants leaned on him as a pass rusher, and he showed he can win off the edge as well as he fills gaps. An offense can’t account for him the same way two plays in a row, and that’s why he’s being talked about as the Defensive Rookie of the Year favorite.

Similarity Comp: Micah Parsons

The obvious comparison is Micah Parsons, and for once the obvious one is right. Parsons came into the league as a big, fast off-ball linebacker, and Dallas quickly found that his best position was wherever the quarterback was. Reese is following the same path. He’s a top-five pick with the size to rush the passer and the speed to cover ground in space, and he’s already showing up in the backfield more often as the season goes on. If New York keeps moving him around, Parsons-level production is a realistic goal.
