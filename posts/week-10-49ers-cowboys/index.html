Game of the Week

San Francisco 46, Dallas 40

By Adam Long/Week 10

San Francisco 49ers8-1
vs
Dallas Cowboys4-6
AT&T Stadium, Arlington, Texas

As an NFL football fan, what is your favorite part of watching games? Is it following along to see if your fantasy football team is going to win your first game of the season? Is it watching some of the best athletes on the planet defying physics in performing superhuman feats week in and week out? Is it touchdowns? Mine’s touchdowns. I like offense. Sue me. I’ve got a lot of respect for real hard-nosed defensive play (this is reflected in my power rankings every week if you don’t believe me), and I do get a real kick out of watching guys like Flores or Spagnuolo put rookie quarterbacks into meat grinders. But me? I’m a yards guy. A real Big XII football guy, through and through. Remember that game between Texas Tech Mahomes and Oklahoma Mayfield like ten years ago when both QBs combined for like 50,000 yards in one game? That was awesome. One time the Cougs even lost to the UCLA Bruins after they scored 63 points. And that was less awesome, but still pretty fun to watch. But this is the NFL! Teams don’t do stuff like that in this league. Total points scored changes from year to year but generally hovers between 44-46 per game. That is a far cry from the offensive explosions we see weekly in the NCAA.

On Sunday afternoon, the Cowboys and 49ers did not get the memo. A 46-40 instant classic, and this one was one of the only games of the week that had me glued to my television set. I’m not going to run through a traditional score-by-score recap on this one, because it would take too long, but I will give you some of the statlines that might make your eyes pop. 49ers QB Brock Purdy threw for 389 yards on 22 completions with three touchdown passes. Cowboys QB Dak Prescott, not to be outdone, threw for 343 yards on 30 completions with five touchdown passes of his own. Three different San Francisco players ran for 50+ yards, and WR Deebo Samuel had a house call from 70 yards out. In a fun flip-flop stat, RB Christian McCaffrey had a 74-yard receiving touchdown, as well as a 66 yard receiving touchdown! That’s three (count em!) 65+ yard tuddies, enough for an entire week of Long Touchdown Enjoyers like myself.

The Dallas offense, or the GOTI (Greatest Offense There Is), as they like to call themselves, had less explosiveness than San Francisco but were no less effective, finally living up to their self-aggrandizing moniker. WR George Pickens stole the show for the Cowboys. He matched McCaffrey with three touchdowns himself to go along with his 145 receiving yards. WR CeeDee Lamb, who has been one of the league’s best players for the last half-decade, had seven catches for 72 yards and a gravity-defying score of his own. But enough of boring you with stats (unless you live for this shit, as I do), let’s move on to the other side of the coin.

Both of these defenses were pretty awful on Sunday. In Dallas’ case, their defense was historically so. The Cowboys’ defense allowed 586 yards, the most yards they had ever allowed at home in their entire franchise’s tenure. Ha! This has been one of the worst units in football over the last few years, and Sunday was their shit-tinted magnum opus. Defensive coordinator Christian Parker needs to set sail, but far be it from Jerry to admit malpractice, so the Cowboys will likely keep Parker on staff until 2027. Dallas falls to 4-6, still alive in one of the league’s worst divisions, but the walls are closing in.

For San Francisco, you can’t really be too worried about improving to 8-1. Especially considering that you nearly dropped a 50 burger without your best receiver. The defense was definitely a problem, specifically down the stretch (Dallas scored 21 points in the fourth quarter), and this is turning into a worrying trend. A few weeks ago, San Francisco had the Washington Commanders beaten and bloodied before halftime, but a second-half meltdown almost allowed Washington to pull off what would’ve been one of the most shocking upsets of the season. Kyle Shanahan and Raheem Morris need to figure out how to close teams out more effectively but I would like to stress the fact that 8-1 is 8-1. The 49ers are the best team in the NFC, and it appears as though the road to the Super Bowl will run through Santa Clara, California. This is, of course, contingent upon intradivisional cannibalization within the NFC North, something we have all grown accustomed to at this point.

86 points in a game is the most scored all season. Can we get to 100? Only time will tell!
