Thursday Night Recap

Caleb Williams is the Best Quarterback in the NFL Right Now

By Adam Long/Week 7

Okay, okay, I know what you’re thinking. Adam, you all say in unison, you’re a day late with your weekly Thursday Night Recap! Well, contrary to popular belief, I do in fact have a job. So I would like to start by apologizing for my transgressions against all you loyal 3rd & Long fans out there. Luckily, we have a whole crew of talented contributors aboard this ship now, so the plan as of now is to have a new article for every day of the week! We’re out here doing this shit for the love of the game, and the love of the game alone.

Anyway, what was I talking about? Oh right, there was a football game on Thursday night! And despite what you may have heard about Thursday Night Football, this one was actually pretty entertaining. We had a real high stakes, high drama matchup on our hands in the Windy City, where 2024 first-overall pick QB Caleb Williams led his team against 2024 third-overall pick, Patriots QB Drake Maye. Both teams were red-hot coming into this primetime contest, Maye and the Patriots had ripped off three straight wins to get their record to 4-2, and the Bears were able to weather Williams’ hamstring injury to keep the ship afloat at 4-2 as well. Both teams are looking to replicate their successes from 2025, in which both second year quarterbacks took massive leaps and propelled their teams deep into January (and even February, in Maye’s case).

Now, let me go off on a bit of a tangent here. Seeing as you can read (nice work!), you probably read the title of this article. I really think that Williams is the NFL’s best quarterback? A guy who’s missed three and a half games this year due to injury? Adam, you ask me incredulously, have you ever heard of a guy called Allen? Jackson? Stroud? Burrow? Yes. I have heard of all of these gentlemen. Wouldn’t really be much of an amateur sportswriter if I hadn’t. Why do I think a guy who has won one singular playoff game is better than a guy named Mahomes who’s won three rings? Two words. Eye test. When I watch Caleb Williams play football, I watch something that is difficult for me to describe. Call it arm talent, call it leadership, call it the It-Factor. I know there are other signal callers in the league who have these intangibles as well, but there’s something else with the guy who wears #18 in Chicago. I’m going to say this now, so I have it in writing, but Caleb Williams is going to win multiple Super Bowls and firmly cement himself as the greatest player in the history of the Chicago Bears. Respect to Urlacher, Butkus, Ditka (RIP), Sayers, and Sweetness, but I don’t think the ceiling for a Bears player has ever been higher. He has every skill necessary for greatness. I’m saying all of this, shouting from the rooftops, and you can’t chalk it up to bias because I’m not even a Bears fan. When I watch Williams play, I see early-2010s Aaron Rodgers.

You probably also clicked on this article to get a recap of what actually happened in the game between New England and Chicago. So I guess I owe that to you, although what I really want to do is keep talking about how good Caleb Williams is. Good football gets me fired up, man. I feel like Gruden out here. Anyway, Maye and the Patriots received the opening kickoff, which they promptly turned over on downs due to a sack courtesy of elite edge rusher Kyler Gordon. Now, despite that last sentence, Kyler Gordon actually plays corner, which is one of the most interesting stories of the young NFL season. Caleb and Da Bears rolled down the field and scored on a field goal, and also added on an RB De’Andre Swift touchdown run to make the lead 10-0 at the end of one. Maye answered right back with a touchdown drive of his own, scoring on a gutsy seven-yard rush to convert on 2nd & Goal. Add in a Bears field goal, an absolute meatball from Maye throwing into the end zone, and you’ve got a recipe for 13-7 Chicago halfway through the second. Chicago WR Luther Burden III was fully unleashed in this game, going for 130 yards and a score, and he earned that score on a 24-yard screen pass. Now this is usually the sentence where I say something like “Both teams went to halftime looking ahead to the second half” or something boring like that, but not in this one. 0:16 left in the half. Maye hits RB Rhamondre Stevenson on a check down, and he jogs through the Bears’ defense to score from 48 yards out. He’s not even the fast Patriots running back! Chicago, what the hell was going on out there! Ben Johnson nearly had an aneurysm on the sideline, and the teams headed to their respective locker rooms to the tune of 20-14.

Dueling touchdowns started the second half, one from Williams to TE Colston Loveland, and one from Maye to TE Eli Raridon. Raridon has been excellent so far in his young career, and I was especially excited to see him score immediately following a boneheaded penalty that he committed. Bears’ K Cairo Santos was bitten by the doink bug and couldn’t convert from 53 yards out. Maye and the Pats went three and out, and Chicago got the ball back, up six points with a chance to put the icing on the cake. There were two plays on this drive in particular that cemented the idea in my head for the title of this article. Both were third down conversions from Williams to WR Rome Odunze, where Caleb attempted and completed two throws that defied all reason. It was pure command of the game, gutsy, gunslinging quarterbacking in its truest sense. Those two throws showed me everything I needed to see from this kid. Now, credit where credit is due only works if the opposite is true. Williams had the Bears in field goal range when he decided to NOT be the best quarterback in the NFL for a snap. He took a bad sack on third down that knocked them out of Santos’ range, so Ben Johnson had a real decision to make. One of the reasons that Johnson is also one of the NFL’s best coaches is that he has the utmost trust in his players. Cairo Santos’ career long was 55 yards up until Thursday night. He had just missed a kick off the upright from 53. This kick to ice the game was from 58. Johnson trusted his guy, and Santos nailed it. There would’ve been a lot of chatter from the peanut gallery had Santos missed that kick and given the ball back to Maye at midfield down six, but luckily for the Bears, they won’t have to hear a peep all week.

So Chicago wins the game and improves their season mark to 5-2. Tough sledding in their division, as Minnesota is undefeated and both Green Bay and Detroit only have two losses themselves. With that being said, give me Chicago in a heartbeat to take the North. You know what, I’m feeling crazy. Give me Chicago to win the NFC.
