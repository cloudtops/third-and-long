Rookie Radar

Jalon Daniels, QB, Tampa Bay Buccaneers

By Noah Labao/Week 5

“You can’t be the thermometer, you have to be the thermostat”

Jalon Daniels

In his first game as an NFL Starter, Daniels completed 19 of 25 for 189 yards and a touchdown, good for a 110.2 passer rating, leaning on Bucky Irving’s 175 scrimmage yards and picking his spots with the kind of moxie you only see once in a blue moon from a free-agent rookie QB. He became just the fourth undrafted rookie to win a primetime game since 2000, and the second to do it on the road. Fitting, for a guy who preaches being the thermostat and not the thermometer: on a hostile Thursday night in a drama, disaster and disgrace filled Jerry-world, Daniels cranked up the heat from his first snap.

College Bio: Kansas Jayhawks (2020 to 2025)

Daniels was the face of Kansas football’s revival. A second-team All-Big 12 pick in 2022, he engineered the Jayhawks’ 5-0 start that season before a shoulder injury cost him four games, and he closed his career ranked second in program history in both passing yards (9,282) and passing touchdowns (67) behind Jayhawk legend, Todd Reesing. A true dual-threat, he hurt defenses with his legs as readily as his arm across a long six-year college run, becoming the kind of program cornerstone that made his draft-weekend snub all the more surprising.

High School Recruiting Profile: Lawndale HS (Lawndale, CA)

The overlooked theme starts early with Daniels. A three-star prospect out of Lawndale, California, he was rated something like the No. 61 dual-threat athlete in the 2020 class, and Kansas was the only Power Five program to offer him a scholarship, a connection owed to then-Jayhawks staff Les Miles and Brent Dearmon. A growth spurt before his junior year allowed him to come into full form: he led Lawndale to a 14-2 record with 2,351 passing yards, 940 rushing yards, and 36 total touchdowns. The blue bloods never came calling, which makes the primetime stage he’s on now all the sweeter.

Scheme Fit: Why He Works in Tampa Bay

Tampa Bay’s offense doesn’t ask its fill-in to be a hero, and that suits Daniels perfectly. With Bucky Irving powering the ground game and a defense keeping things close, and Egbuka & Godwin with the experience to account for inexperienced throws or decisions, Daniels’ job is to be a maestro, exactly what he did in Dallas. His version of mobility adds a dimension that Mayfield may be losing due to age or style of running, letting Bowles lean on designed movement and bootlegs, and his poise under primetime pressure suggests the moment isn’t too big. He’ll keep the seat warm as the starter while Mayfield heals, and on this evidence Tampa Bay won’t be giving games away in the interim.

Similarity Comp: Gardner Minshew

The closest template is Gardner Minshew (biased, but the facts add up). An overlooked, late-draft-or-undrafted gamer who seized an injury opening and played with far more focus and command than his pedigree suggested. Like Minshew, Daniels wins with confidence, mobility, and ball security rather than prototype measurables, the kind of backup who keeps a team afloat and occasionally steals one it had no business winning. To me, that’s always what organizations want in their backup quarterback. If he keeps protecting the ball the way he did against Dallas, he’s exactly the sort of feel-good bridge starter we may see bouncing around from org-to-org, which isn’t a bad career at all in the grand scheme of the national football league.

  • Jacob Rodriguez, LB, Miami Dolphins - Week 5: another 11-tackle afternoon against Cincinnati to push his league-leading total to 57 on the season, the kind of tackle-machine production that has been the throughline since his Texas Tech days.
