Hawks Talk

By Nate Kjormoe/Week 8

“It was the best of times, it was the worst of times.”

Charles Dickens, A Tale of Two Cities (1859)

As Dickens finished that iconic verse, it was the winter of despair. Now, how did we get to this winter of despair? I’ll tell you. Coming off the highest accomplishment in the sport, winning the coveted Super Bowl Championship and being named world champions, you’d think the Seattle Seahawks would be on the highest of highs. But that has not been the story in the 2026 season. If I had been told at the beginning of the season that the Seahawks would be 4-4 coming out of Week 8, I would have laughed in your face.

And my laughter would not have been unfounded. As the defending Super Bowl champs, we did what most champions can’t do.

The most common theme of a Super Bowl-winning team is typically turnover. We live in a copycat league, and teams want to replicate what successful programs do. A trophy normally results in other teams calling for coaches and trading for players to absorb some of that championship pedigree. And this certainly happened to the Seahawks after their win. The Seahawks lost running back Kenneth Walker III, along with Boye Mafe, Coby Bryant, Riq Woolen, and Dareke Young. Out of those five, only Walker and Bryant were starters in the Super Bowl.

MEANING… THE SEATTLE FUCKING SEAHAWKS RETURNED 48/53 PLAYERS AND 88% OF TOTAL SNAPS.

Returning this many players after a Super Bowl win is nothing less than rare. So, what the fuck happened? Well, if I were one to point fingers, which I certainly am, I would say there’s only one clear answer. So, let’s examine the facts.

Our defense is still elite. Coming into this game, the Hawks held teams to a historic level of 233.9 yards per game through seven games. This was on pace to rank #1 among defenses this century! We ranked 1st in total yards allowed, 1st in passing yards allowed, 4th in points allowed, and 6th in rushing yards allowed. A dominant defense that arguably is beating its predecessor, the 2025 Dark Side, who, as mentioned, just won a Super Bowl. Even with the Minnesota Vikings’ dominant run, the Seahawks were still averaging 38 fewer yards allowed per game. And coming off a performance where they held the Kansas City Chiefs offense out of the red area for an entire game and held the leading rusher in the NFL to 36 rushing yards (yes, that was K9, let’s not bring it up). This defense has clearly displayed that it is the #1 defense in the league.

So, if it’s not the defense, where does the finger lie?

As previously mentioned, the Hawks returned 48/53 players from the Super Bowl-winning team, specifically returning 14/15 major offensive contributors. The offensive line at the end of last season ranked 15th overall and 10th in run blocking. The WR room had the #1 ranked player in Jaxon Smith-Njigba, and a running back room with 2 of the top 10 ranked players (K9 2nd, Zach Charbonnet 7th) per PFF grading. Now get this, because here’s the fucking kicker. Sam Darnold finished the 2025 NFL season ranked 9th in PFF overall grade among qualifying QBs.

FUCKING 9th!!

So, we return 14/15 offensive players, have previously ranked top-10 units in almost every aspect, and we are 2-6… Ah fuck, sorry, I meant 4-4. Feels like 2-6, let’s be honest, and it very well could be. We beat the New England Patriots with a backup QB, who, at the time, let’s be honest, were not up to form (now 5-3 and playing well). And we beat the Chiefs, which, up until this Bears performance, felt like the Hawks’ true form, but now feels like the outlier of the season. It feels like an outlier mainly because our offense was actually able to play complementary to our defense for once this season.

Our offense playing complementary football is the outlier.

Through eight games, our offense has not played good football. Hell, it’s barely played football, period. Going into this game, we were 23rd in run success rate, 20th in red-area conversions, 21st in third-down conversions, and 27th in turnovers. We ranked in the bottom 10 in most meaningful categories on offense. A strong contrast to our defense. Our only option is throwing to JSN, who, fuck it, why not? He’s the best WR in the league. But overall, this has been an abysmal display of offensive football thus far this season.

Yes, Sam Darnold has not performed well. I have been a huge Darnold supporter through this season. Hell, the man didn’t turn the ball over once throughout the playoffs. He was decisive last season and, like I mentioned, finished 9th in the league. This year, however, he has looked lost. Aside from the turnovers, and that is a large “aside” because it’s a fucking problem, Sam looks genuinely uncomfortable. He has quick feet, is out of rhythm, and is missing his steps. This is resulting in him missing his reads, missing his throws, and throwing picks. He has not played well. But to Sam’s credit, he is playing under a 42.3% pressure rate (5th highest among qualified QBs) and has taken 14 sacks this season, tied for 12th most. The point is, Sam has not been put in a position for success this season, especially with the disastrous run game this year. We can’t give up on Sam just yet, or we’re just as bad as the Vikings. And we all know they’re a fraud of a franchise this year.

Nate, wtf are we talking about right now? Great point, got lost in the ramble.

So, where am I leading this? Essentially, we took something beautiful and nice and fun, and in one offseason, one man turned this into a disastrous giant turd. And who is that man?

Brian Fucking Fleury.

This Bears game was disgusting to watch. There simply was no game plan. For fuck’s sake, who runs the ball back-to-back with six minutes left in the fourth quarter… DOWN 10!! The protection plan has been, and still is, nonexistent. Especially with the pressure rate in mind I mentioned earlier. The game plan was inconsistent, to say the least. I genuinely believe a high schooler playing Madden could have called a better game than Brian Fleury in this performance. Not to mention, we just traded for an eight-time Pro Bowl left guard, and we ran 47.1% to the right side of the ball, and only 23.5% went to Quenton Nelson’s side (4/17 directly behind Nelson). Takes a real genius to call that.

And let’s make one thing abundantly clear. This is not some knee-jerk reaction to one embarrassing Monday Night Football performance. We are EIGHT weeks into the season, and the same offensive deficiencies have plagued this team since Week 1. No rhythm, no consistency, no identity. By Week 6, this offense ranked 25th in EPA per play and 30th in rushing EPA per carry. The coaching staff publicly acknowledged these struggles, promising adjustments. And for one glorious week against Kansas City, it looked like they had finally figured something out. Then came Chicago. One third-down conversion in eleven attempts, another nonexistent rushing attack, and an offense that looked completely lost. At what point does this stop being an execution problem and start becoming a coaching problem? Eight weeks of the same mistakes, eight weeks of failed adjustments, and eight weeks of watching a championship-caliber roster struggle to move the football. That’s not a coincidence. That’s a pattern. And patterns start at the top.

Occam’s Razor…

The simplest explanation is usually the most likely answer. The Seattle Seahawks have returned almost all their players, even adding new pieces for strength. We run almost the same scheme and have maintained most of our coaching staff. Yet we are worse in every aspect of the offense (JSN excluded). The common denominator? Brian Fleury. The offense looks confused, out of rhythm, and without a plan. Much like a ship without a captain.

We have a true captain, Mike Macdonald. And it’s time for him to make the tough call.

Fleury needs to go, or at least step down from play-calling responsibilities. Although firing an OC midseason rarely results in a successful turnaround, does anyone truly believe we would do any better with Fleury? Right now, Mike needs to send a message. He needs to show his defense he supports them by not blaming them for the offense’s shortcomings. He needs to show his offense this mediocre performance will not go unpunished. And he needs to shift this downward spiral.

Now, I know what you’re thinking. Nate, you can’t just fire your offensive coordinator halfway through the season and expect everything to magically fix itself. And you’re right. But here’s the thing: we have options. Jake Peetz, our current passing game coordinator and quarterbacks coach, served as LSU’s offensive coordinator and play-caller in 2021. Justin Outten, our run game coordinator, served as the Denver Broncos’ offensive coordinator in 2022 and even took over play-calling duties for their final two games. Hell, both of these guys have more experience calling offensive plays than Fleury had when we handed him the keys to a Super Bowl-winning offense. And who says we even have to fire Fleury? Strip him of play-calling responsibilities, hand the headset to Peetz, and let Fleury focus on game planning and preparation. Is that guaranteed to fix this offense? Absolutely fucking not. But doing the same thing for another nine weeks and expecting different results isn’t exactly a winning strategy either. At some point, Macdonald has to make a change. Because right now, standing still might be the worst decision of all.

There is so much to fix, and moving into Week 9, there’s not a lot of time to fix it. Can our defense hold its own? That’s not a question in my mind. Can this offense turn it around and play well enough to support their team through the season and into the playoffs? A different question entirely, and certainly not one I’d like to answer at this time.

Hopefully yes. Likely no. But there is time.

Ya Boi, Nathanael Alan Kjormoe
