Bake or Shake

The Good, the Bad and the Ugly

By Nate Kjormoe/Week 8

The Good, the Bad and the Ugly

Sounds like a great intro, right? I thought so. Only one problem. There wasn’t a good, there wasn’t a bad, hell, there wasn’t even an ugly. There really wasn’t anything to speak of in this Week 8 performance from Baker Mayfield. Probably because my guy only threw 3 passes the whole game.

Yes, you heard me, 3 passing attempts, the whole damn game!

Now, if you don’t follow football or watch every slate like I do, you’d probably think this was a disastrous SHAKE by Baker, and the Bucs lost in an embarrassing fashion.

I mean, if you don’t watch football, why the hell are you reading this? I appreciate the support regardless, but I digress.

What may surprise you is this isn’t a SHAKE! Now, is it a BAKE? That is the question at hand. With a stat line of 0/3 completions, 0 passing yards, 0 touchdowns, 4 rushing attempts for 14 yards, and a passer rating of 39.6, this certainly couldn’t be a BAKE… Well, if I was able to turn that molehill in my backyard into a mountain the other day, I can definitely turn this stat line into a BAKE!

Just give me a second and let me explain.

For starters, let’s set the stage. In Tampa on Sunday, November 1st, at 1 PM, there was a little thing we in the business like to call… rain. Now, this wasn’t your typical Florida afternoon rain shower. This was a genuinely miserable day as far as weather is concerned. Tampa experienced 30 times its normal daily rainfall with wind speeds up to 25 mph. A true biblical fucking downpour. The field was muddy, the rain was pouring, and the playing conditions were abysmal.

Two quarterbacks, same weather, and two very different outcomes. We’ve already mentioned Baker’s stat line, but what about the other guy? Michael Penix Jr., QB for the Atlanta Falcons, went for 8 completions on 24 attempts for a buck 25, 0 touchdowns, and 4, yes, 4 interceptions! If I cared, I’d tell him to SHAKE it off. But this is BAKE or SHAKE, not Penix or Penis. And after judging that performance, neither one was getting up for the occasion.

Applause? No? Okay, valid… to my point.

Essentially, we had two quarterbacks and two minds. One played to the weather, the other played against it. The Bucs’ game plan was simple. Run the ball. And this was clearly the smart play as the Bucs shut out the Falcons 16-0. Todd Bowles acknowledged that the weather directly dictated the offensive strategy, forcing them to run the ball. And run the ball they did, breaking a single-game rushing record for the Tampa Bay Buccaneers. Rushing for 324 yards on 56 total carries (Baker having 4 of those! Can’t stop this guy from contributing). Now, when the head coach makes a call to absolutely and unequivocally run the ball, can we really fault Baker for that?

No, we can’t. But we can respect the man for putting personal stats aside and doing what’s best for his coach and his team.

So, let’s get back to the man of the hour. Baker did 4 things in this game that really turned this into a BAKE. Number 1, breaking records! Baker made history on this glorious Sunday, my friends. History indeed. He was the first quarterback since the 1945 Pittsburgh Steelers to lead his team to a double-digit win without completing a single pass! Along with being the first quarterback to combine for 0 completions and 300+ team rushing yards since 1933. Hot damn, how does he do it? One can only guess. Now, one point of emphasis: Baker actually did complete a pass! After a bobbled ball, Baker recovered it, threw it to Bucky for an amazing display of playmaking ability. Bucky then proceeded to fumble the ball in the back of the end zone.

Now, “technically” this was a backward lateral, so it didn’t count as a pass/completion. But tomato tomato, it was still a damn good play by Baker the Playmaker Mayfield.

My second point for Baker BAKING is simple. 2/3 of Baker’s passes were on target but unfortunately dropped by the WRs. I’ve made the point of Baker getting poor support from his WRs before, so I won’t go down that road again. But those drops did add to the adjustment call by Todd Bowles to just run the ball. And those drops were not due to poor accuracy by Baker in a downpour.

My third point. Baker showed true leadership in this game. In his postgame press conference, Baker discussed changing plays to shotgun and pistol formations to make the wet snaps easier to receive. He did this after that bobbled ball I mentioned earlier. This not only made it easier to handle snaps, but allowed for better clock management and clearer running lanes. Just the kind of adjustment you’d expect from a savvy vet like Baker!

My 4th and final point, and my clear favorite, was a fucking block. Yes, you heard me. A block. From a quarterback. That is the main example of why this was an excellent performance. Not 300 yards passing or 5 TD throws. A simple football play. Because hey, Baker is a simple football guy! And on a simple football run, Baker did what few quarterbacks would even consider doing.

Late in the third quarter, Bucs running back Kenny Gainwell broke out for a deep rush into the second level. As Gainwell cut back across the open field, Baker took off. Turning from quarterback to the lead damn blocker, Mr. Mayfield met Jessie Bates III around the 10-yard line. But he didn’t stop after contact, no sir, he continued to run those feet. Blocking Bates through the end zone and out of bounds. Sitting Jessie Bates straight on his ass into a puddle. Not only did Baker block this dude into the ground, but he got up and started scrapping with him!

Now, if you can seriously find another example of a modern quarterback putting this amount of effort in for his guys, becoming a lead blocker and blocking a starting safety into the fucking floor, I will end this BAKE or SHAKE column today. But you can’t. Because Baker is truly one of a kind.

Now, jokes aside for a moment. I truly love watching this man play football. This is not the first time Baker has been a lead blocker, not the first time he’s scrapped for his boys, and it will not be the last, I promise you that! Baker is a true football player, and the NFL is a better place with Baker in it.

There are plenty of doubters of Baker. I’ve spoken to many of you since starting this column (you know who you are…) and there are many more I haven’t spoken to, but I know are out there. Baker has been doubted since the day he stepped onto that gridiron. From a walk-on at Texas Tech, to a walk-on at Oklahoma, to winning the Heisman, being selected number one overall. Leading the Browns to their first playoff victory in 26 years. Falling from grace before leading one of the greatest 4th-quarter comebacks in NFL history for the Rams. So, you can doubt Baker all you want, but one thing you cannot deny is that no one plays harder or will put their neck on the line like Baker does. And for those of you who can watch this man play and not get a smile on your face, I truly pity you.

From the great words of Pete Weber:

“Hate me or love me, you still watched. That’s all you could do.”

Pete Weber

And I can’t think of a better quote to describe Baker.

Cut it, print it, take it to the fucking bank. I don’t care what you do.

But that’s a fucking BAKE!

Ya boi, Nathanael Alan Kjormoe
