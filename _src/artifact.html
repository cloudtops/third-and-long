2026–2027 NFL Preseason Report

By Adam Long

As the colors of the leaves begin to change and the suffocating heat and smoke of our Pacific Northwest summer finally start to abate, the sports-minded of us begin to turn our attention once again to our television sets. After a long and arduous offseason, the Football Gods have finally again blessed our homes and our hearts with a primetime matchup between two league heavyweights in a rematch of last season’s championship bout. Tonight, the NFL season begins, but I invite you to take a look with me at the larger picture of the league as a whole. Without further ado, I present to you the 2026-2027 NFL Preseason Report/Prediction.

AFC

North

1

Cincinnati Bengals

2025
6-11
Breakout Candidate
DE Boye Mafe

Have the Bengals become the new Cowboys? Despite missing out on postseason festivities each year since 2022, the Who Dey faithful remain stalwart in their optimism season in and season out. Is it the Bengal’s year? Maybe. The here and now may be the best they’ve looked in the preseason since 2022, as there are few questions about their elite offense and ability to produce fireworks. QB Joe Burrow returns from a nasty turf toe injury that sidelined him for a significant portion of 2025, and WR’s Ja’Marr Chase and Tee Higgins look to capitalize on playing a full campaign with their generational signal caller. The main reason for the Bengals success this season will come from plugging defensive holes with the additions of DT Dexter Lawrence, DT Jonathan Allen, and DE Boye Mafe.

2

Baltimore Ravens

2025
8-9
Breakout Candidate
WR Ja’Kobi Lane (R)

There are a lot of question marks surrounding the Ravens this season as we head into 2026. Most notably, a changing of the guard has occurred in the Head Coach’s seat, with former Chargers defensive coordinator Jesse Minter taking the reins from the long-tenured John Harbaugh, who has sought greener pastures in the Meadowlands. Baltimore’s success has always been predicated upon their power run game, and the main offensive question is whether or not all-world QB Lamar Jackson will be able to return to MVP form. Defensively, the backfield returns All-Pro safety Kyle Hamilton, whose 2025 campaign was unfortunately marred by injury, and the unit as a whole should be significantly improved from last year with Minter calling the shots.

3

Pittsburgh Steelers

2025
10-7
Breakout Candidate
WR Germie Bernard (R)

The Mike Tomlin Experience has finally come to a conclusion in the Steel City, after 19 seasons straight finishing above .500. Lack of postseason success led the Steelers front office to pursue someone who they believe can finally get the job done in January, so they hired…wait, Mike McCarthy? Huh. Anyway, McCarthy gets a reunion with his long-lost QB Aaron Rodgers, the ageless wonder who has committed to this year being his farewell tour. He will be supported by WR D.K. Metcalf, RB Jaylen Warren, and TE Pat Freiermuth. Key additions to the offensive squad include last year’s breakout star RB Rico Dowdle and WR Michael Pittman Jr. Pittsburgh looks to rebound defensively from a campaign that saw them ranked towards the basement of the league last season and have added much-needed depth in the box and secondary, headlined by the acquisitions of CB Jalen Ramsey and S Jaquan Brisker.

4

Cleveland Browns

2025
5-12
Breakout Candidate
RB Quinshon Judkins

Yeah, probably not much of a surprise here. Believeland enters 2026 in quarterback purgatory yet again, as new HC Todd Monken has chosen to delegate offensive responsibilities to All-Around Great Guy and Teammate QB Deshaun Watson. Much to the chagrin of Browns’ fans, who I would imagine would’ve liked to see the nod given to mercurial second-year signal caller QB Shedeur Sanders. Regardless of who is running the offense, firepower remains low, although young bright spots RB Quinshon Judkins and TE Harold Fannin Jr should be able to get Cleveland on the scoreboard with some sense of semi-regularity. Defense, historically the Browns’ calling card, suffered a massive offseason blow with the departure of Canton-bound DE Myles Garrett, but the unit as a whole should still be serviceable. DE Jared Verse, LB Carson Schwesinger, and CB Denzel Ward will lead this squad to a top 15 ranking in DVOA by season’s end, even if the team wins five games or less.

East

1

Buffalo Bills

2025
12-5
Breakout Candidate
DT Deone Walker

A bounce back season for the Bills seems to be in order. After another absolute postseason heartbreaker at the hands of Sean Payton, Buffalo has finally made a significant change by ousting HC Sean McDermott (one of the winningest bosses in franchise history), and replacing him with former offensive coordinator Joe Brady. The offense has added playmaking WR DJ Moore from Chicago, and Buffalo boasts one of the best QB/RB duos in the league with QB Josh Allen and RB James Cook. The defense remains largely the same, (although the introduction of a 3-4 scheme to the unit should shake things up somewhat) but the addition of DE Bradley Chubb should help out DE Gregory Rousseau, and a pass rush that was ranked 27th in PRWR in 2025.

2

New England Patriots

2025
14-3
Breakout Candidate
WR Kyle Williams

After using the genie in the bottle that team owner Robert Kraft found in a Foxborough storage closet back in the year 2000 to wish for multiple Super Bowl trophies and the greatest football player of all time, it seems Kraft has used the genie again to wish for a return to glory. With one of the quickest rebuilds in league history culminating in a Super Bowl appearance last year, New England is one of the most exciting and surprising clubs in football. How long did we have, maybe five years of Patriot irrelevance? QB Drake Maye returns from an MVP-caliber campaign in 2025 and should be much improved behind a better offensive line and the addition of WR AJ Brown. The defense, stalwart in 2025, adds key pieces to the mix in DT Milton Williams and S Kevin Byard III. This year is New England’s opportunity to quell the suspicion that the main reason for their success last season was their schedule.

3

New York Jets

2025
3-14
Breakout Candidate
TE Kenyon Sahdiq (R)

Yep, that’s right, you’re reading this correctly. The J-E-T-S will not be in the cellar of the AFC East this season! HC Aaron Glenn begins year two in Gotham after a disastrous 2025 campaign, but will take over defensive playcalling duties for a unit that should improve over last year with the addition of journeyman S Minkah Fitzpatrick. On offense, the Jets return former first-round QB Geno Smith, who looks to improve on a 2025 season in Vegas where he led the league in interceptions. WR Garrett Wilson returns from injury, and new Oregon product TE Kenyon Sahdiq should be an immediate difference maker for the other team from New York.

4

Miami Dolphins

2025
7-10
Breakout Candidate
QB Malik Willis

The Miami Dolphins should end the 2026 season with the only real stat that matters in this league: the first overall draft pick and the right to draft future Hall of Famer WR Jeremiah Smith (Yeah, I’m calling it now. Have you seen that dude play?). Looking more immediately, Miami should be an absolute dumpster fire this year, as the team has virtually no bright spots outside of RB De’Von Achane. I honestly don’t really have a lot to say about this club, aside from that if your favorite team plays against the Dolphins this year, congrats on the win. Look for Miami to win anywhere between one and three games this season.

AFC

South

1

Houston Texans

2025
12-5
Breakout Candidate
G Keylan Rutledge

After beginning the 2025 season with a record of 0-3, Houston ended up winning a playoff game. QB CJ Stroud should likely improve this season, as further regression seems impossible after a dazzling rookie season followed by two years of sub-par play. It seems like it really can’t get worse for Houston offensively than what it looked like in New England in the playoffs last year, where Stroud was clearly seeing ghosts. Luckily for the Texans, they really only need their offense to score two touchdowns a game, and maybe a field goal, in order to win 12-14 games in 2026. Houston boasts, statistically speaking, one of the best defensive units to play football this century. An embarrassment of riches blesses the Texans across all positions, highlighted by the DE tandem of Will Anderson Jr and Danielle Hunter (27 sacks in 2025(!)), and the secondary pairing of CB Derek Stingley Jr and S Jalen Pitre. If Houston’s offense can simply be middle of the road in 2026, this team has a real shot at a title.

2

Jacksonville Jaguars

2025
13-4
Breakout Candidate
RB Bhayshul Tuten

The Jaguars, one of the NFL’s most improved teams last year, are coming in hot off the tail of an AFC South division title and a 13-win season. After losing a heartbreaker to Buffalo in the playoffs, Jacksonville looks to replicate last year’s regular season success with a more balanced offensive attack (Enter RB Chris Rodriguez Jr to complement young emergent talent RB Bhayshul Tuten), and finally look for QB Trevor Lawrence to play at an elite level for an entire season. Lawrence was great at times last year, but for Jacksonville to finally get over the hump they need an MVP-caliber season from the Clemson product.

3

Indianapolis Colts

2025
8-9
Breakout Candidate
WR Alec Pierce

I foresee the Indianapolis Colts having another season similar to 2025, albeit without the red-hot start they experienced behind the excellent play of QB Daniel Jones and the ridiculous first half of the season from RB Jonathan Taylor. The Colts’ season fell off the rails after Indiana Jones’ injury, and the team was unable to really get anything going after the midway point. The main question mark with Indianapolis this year is their receiving corps, which following the departure of WR Michael Pittman Jr to Pittsburgh lacks any sort of significant firepower. Jones will look to WR Alec Pierce as his number one target, who is an unquestioned deep threat but we haven’t seen Pierce in a number one role before. The defense is largely unchanged and will likely be a middle-of-the-road unit.

4

Tennessee Titans

2025
3-14
Breakout Candidate
WR Wan’Dale Robinson

I have a lot of hope for the Titans this season, I just think the AFC South is going to be too competitive in 2026 for a team that is still clearly rebuilding to throw any sort of wrench into things. Second year QB Cam Ward will take a massive leap forward due to the additions of first-round WR Carnell Tate, who dazzled at Ohio State last season, and WR Wan’Dale Robinson, who is sneaky underrated and will be a PPR monster for fantasy owners this year as Tate acclimatizes to the speed of the pro game. Tennessee’s defense was surprisingly stout over the first half of the year before regressing to finish with a 28th-overall ranking. DT Jeffrey Simmons is still an elite playmaker and will anchor a defense that will likely improve somewhat.

West

1

Denver Broncos

2025
14-3
Breakout Candidate
WR Pat Bryant

It is certainly no steaming hot take to ascertain that, barring the injury to QB Bo Nix at the end of a divisional playoff game last year, Denver almost would’ve certainly beat New England in the AFC Championship and advanced to face Seattle in the Super Bowl. However, that was then, and this is now, and Nix looks to be fully healthy and ready to shoulder the burden of being one of the league’s most talked about Super Bowl favorites. Denver’s offense was good enough last year, but the addition of WR Jaylen Waddle gives the unit a tremendous boost in firepower. The offensive line remains one of the best in football. The defense is the heart and soul of this team, however, as DE Nik Bonitto and former DPOTY CB Pat Surtain II anchor what was the second-overall ranked defense in football last season. Denver should be able to get over the hump and compete for a Lombardi Trophy in 2026.

2

Los Angeles Chargers

2025
11-6
Breakout Candidate
RB Omarion Hampton

2026 is make it or break it time for the Bolts. Despite being everyone’s preseason darling for what feels like the last half-decade, the Chargers have failed to make any real waves into January despite the success of their golden boy QB Justin Herbert. The really fun addition that should actually translate into more offensive success this year is offensive coordinator Mike McDaniel, who has spent most of this preseason ooh’ing and aah’ing at Herbert’s raw ability as a playmaker. C Tyler Biadasz and TE David Njoku highlight the offseason moves, and the defense should remain stout as well under the tutelage of HC Jim Harbaugh. I foresee the Chargers experiencing relative success this season, 10+ wins and a playoff victory don’t seem too far out of the realm of possibility

3

Kansas City Chiefs

2025
6-11
Breakout Candidate
CB Mansoor Delane (R)

Man, it feels so good to be able to put the Chiefs at third in their division for a second year in a row. I am by no means an overt Kansas City hater, but it just gets boring seeing the same few teams compete year in and year out into February. Luckily for us, the Chiefs’ dynasty appears to have ended last year after future Hall of Fame QB Patrick Mahomes II finally looked human and was largely unable to capitalize in situations (3rd-and-manageables, red area opportunities) that he has dominated for most of the last decade. TE Travis Kelce returns but should largely be an afterthought. Look for the WR tandem of Rashee Rice and Xavier Worthy to bust some seams, and the addition of 2025’s Super Bowl MVP RB Kenneth Walker III to light up the scoreboard for KC. Even if the offense returns to form, the defense has experienced such significant losses in CB’s Jaylen Watson and Trent McDuffie Jr both departing for the City of Angels that the Chiefs will again miss the playoffs and be largely non-competitive.

4

Las Vegas Raiders

2025
3-14
Breakout Candidate
LB Nakobe Dean

After the dismal season that was 2025, the Raiders should actually have a lot to be excited about entering 2026. After spending their first overall draft pick on reigning Heisman winner QB Fernando Mendoza, who led a scrappy Indiana Hoosier team to their first-ever national championship, Raiders fans are very intrigued to see what the future holds. Although Mendoza will likely ride pine behind QB Kirk Cousins (which is, in my opinion, the best thing a team can do for a young, promising quarterback), Vegas’ offense actually looks kind of dangerous with TE Brock Bowers and RB Ashton Jeanty looking to come into their own in their third and second seasons respectively. The defense added LB Kwity Paye and LB Nakobe Dean in the offseason, who will help this young unit gel alongside elite pass rusher DE Maxx Crosby.

Detroit Lions

2025
9-8
Breakout Candidate
T Blake Miller (R)

Largely underrated it seems entering 2026 based on a very middling campaign in 2025, the Detroit Lions are still one of the league’s elite teams. Although missing out on the playoffs in 2025 with a 9-8 record in a very competitive division definitely hurt, this roster is built more effectively than most other teams in the league and their success in 2026 will reflect that. The offense is loaded. QB Jared Goff has been on a quiet heater over the last few years, with three straight seasons of 4400+ passing yards and 30+ touchdowns. RB Jahmyr Gibbs is the favorite to win Offensive Player of the Year, and WRs Jameson Williams and Amon-Ra St. Brown complement elite pass-catching TE Sam LaPorta. Add in rookie T Blake Miller in tandem with All Pro T Penei Sewell, and it’s not that far-fetched to assume Detroit can score 35+ points per game. The defense has more question marks, especially with a few secondary pieces starting on the PUP list, but the return of DE Aidan Hutchinson will make a massive impact on the pass rush and the soul of Dan Campbell’s club.

2

Chicago Bears

2025
12-5
Breakout Candidate
DT Kentavius Street

In 2025, QB Caleb Williams proved himself as a player worthy of the first overall pick in 2024 and then some. Williams provided Chicago with playmaking ability at the quarterback position that the NFL’s second-oldest franchise has honestly never seen in its history. Williams led Chicago to a jaw-dropping postseason comeback over the hated Packers, and had Bears fans partying like it was 1985. The Bears’ success largely runs through if Williams will be able to duplicate and build on the gains he made in HC Ben Johnson’s offensive system last year. Holes in the defense will make this more difficult for Caleb and the Bears, but I am drinking the Kool-Aid and I predict Chicago will return to the postseason in 2026.

3

Green Bay Packers

2025
9-7-1
Breakout Candidate
RB MarShawn Lloyd

Such a strange year for Green Bay in 2025. After looking like 20-0 and a Lombardi homecoming were inevitabilities at the start of the season, the Packers crashed back to earth in spectacular fashion after the loss of LB Micah Parsons and QB Jordan Love. If these two key pieces can stay healthy, Green Bay can certainly make a postseason run, but I just have too many questions about the offensive firepower (even after finally spending their first round pick last year on WR Matthew Golden, the Green and Gold remain without a true WR1). The suspension of RB Josh Jacobs is an off-field scandal that does nothing to help HC Matt LaFleur’s team. The defense will have to lean on S Xavier McKinney and LB Zaire Franklin at least through the difficult start of their 2026 schedule as Parsons will begin the year on injured reserve.

4

Minnesota Vikings

2025
9-8
Breakout Candidate
QB Kyler Murray

It feels like a decade ago when the Vikings won 14 games and made the playoffs. It was actually only two years back, but last year’s middling 9-8 campaign led to a lot of people souring on the Purple People Eaters. The experiment with drafting a raw rookie quarterback in JJ McCarthy has been a pretty abject failure, and Minnesota has now turned to the services of veteran QB Kyler Murray to right the ship and get the offense moving back in the right direction. WR Justin Jefferson is still one of the game’s elite playmaking receivers, and TE TJ Hockenson should be able to bail Murray out of some 3rd-and-long situations. Minnesota does actually have one of the league’s best defenses (7th in scoring last season), but the success of this team will certainly depend on whether Murray is able to replicate the magic of his rookie season seven years later.

1

Philadelphia Eagles

2025
11-6
Breakout Candidate
WR Makai Lemon (R)

Rejoice, Eagles faithful, the bad man is gone. After a dominant Super Bowl-winning campaign in 2024, the Eagles crashed spectacularly back to earth last season, a campaign defined largely by the playcalling ineptitude of former offensive coordinator Kevin Patullo. Patullo is out, Sean Mannion is in, and Philly should at least see shades of the 2024 versions of RB Saquon Barkley and QB Jalen Hurts. Hurts will have to rely on the WR tandem of DeVonta Smith and rookie Makai Lemon, as All Pro WR AJ Brown has recently departed for Foxborough. The defense lost a few key pieces in DT Milton Williams, S Reed Blankenship, and LB Nakobe Dean, but the unit is still pretty great and should finish top 10 in defensive rankings.

2

Dallas Cowboys

2025
7-9-1
Breakout Candidate
S Caleb Downs (R)

Look at me, Cowboys fans. Look me in the eye. It’s not your year. The Dallas Cowboys are an enigma. The offense looks primed for another ridiculously successful campaign, boasting a strong running game (RB Javonte Williams), an elite offensive line, one of the best WR corps in the league (CeeDee Lamb and George Pickens), and consistent quarterback play from QB Dak Prescott. The problem with Dallas is that they have the worst defense in football. No amount of moves from owner Jerry Jones last year could salvage what was the worst statistical unit in football. Rookie S Caleb Downs could give Dallas’ defense the adrenaline shot they need to compete, but my prediction is that Dallas averages 35+ points per game and still misses the playoffs.

3

New York Giants

2025
4-13
Breakout Candidate
LB Arvell Reese (R)

The New York Giants may actually be competitive in 2026 following a lot of shakeups in the Big Apple, most notably the addition of HC John Harbaugh. Harbaugh will bring a hard-nosed attitude to a team that has done a great job building their roster through the draft over the last few years, and this year under Harbaugh’s leadership they may surprise a lot of people. The electric offensive combination of second-year players QB Jaxson Dart and RB Cam Skattebo will continue to hit their stride, with help from the return of elite WR Malik Nabers, whose 2025 campaign was cut short prematurely due to injury. On the other side of the ball, the defensive front seven is legitimately terrifying. Brian Burns, Kayvon Thibodeaux, Abdul Carter, and highly touted Arvell Reese make up a pass-rushing unit that is, on paper, probably the most dangerous in football. If Harbaugh can give this team an identity, the Giants should experience more success than expected in 2026.

4

Washington Commanders

2025
5-12
Breakout Candidate
WR Antonio Williams (R)

Sigh. After a disaster of a season for Washington in 2025, a campaign marred by injuries all over both sides of the ball, the road back to success just looks more difficult in 2026. QB Jayden Daniels returns to lead the offense alongside WRs Terry McLaurin and newly acquired Stefon Diggs. RB Ja’Cory Croskey-Merritt and RB Rachaad White will take some pressure off Daniels in the run game, and barring any more catastrophic injuries (T Laremy Tunsil is already lost for the season), this offense should replicate some of the success of Daniels’ stellar rookie campaign in 2024. The defense, which was atrocious last year, has plugged some holes in the acquisitions of DE Odafe Oweh and rookie LB Sonny Styles. This unit should likely be better than 2025, again, barring the bite from the injury bug that the team faced in 2025. The problem for Washington isn’t necessarily the roster, it is the scheduling start to the year that puts the Commanders in an admittedly terrible position, playing a multitude of road games against some of the league’s best teams.

Tampa Bay Buccaneers

2025
8-9
Breakout Candidate
DE Reuben Bain Jr (R)

Tampa Bay rode the hot hand of QB Baker Mayfield to a blazing start out of the gate last season before crashing back to earth over the second half of the year in a campaign that saw them lose eight of their last eleven games. This team should certainly be better than last year, as a lot of the disaster that was the second half of 2025 can be attributed to injuries. Mayfield will lean on the return of WR Chris Godwin as well as the continued emergence of second year WR Emeka Egbuka. The defense should be top 10 this season, led by elite players S Antoine Winfield Jr and DT Vita Vea, as well as the addition of first round pick DE Reuben Bain Jr. Tampa Bay should win this division handily if all goes according to plan.

2

Carolina Panthers

2025
8-9
Breakout Candidate
WR Jalen Coker

I believe in QB Bryce Young. Maybe it's his sunny disposition, maybe it’s his upbeat attitude, or maybe it’s his newfound fiery leadership capabilities, but I foresee Young finally taking the leap that Carolina knew he could make when they selected him with the first overall pick back in 2023. Young finally has playmakers around him, as 2025 first round pick WR Tetairoa McMillan was nothing short of a home run, and WR Jalen Coker continues to come into his own as a very solid WR2. RB Chuba Hubbard will shoulder the load after the departure of 2025 superstar RB Rico Dowdle. The defense last year was right in the middle of the road (16th overall), but should take a massive leap forward with the additions of DE Jaelan Phillips and absurdly underrated LB Devin Lloyd.

3

New Orleans Saints

2025
6-11
Breakout Candidate
DE Chase Young

A difficult team to predict for 2026. New Orleans was a bottom-dweller last season, but I see the Saints improving significantly this year as second-year QB Tyler Shough builds on the success he saw after taking over the offense towards the end of 2025. Newly acquired RB Travis Etienne will provide a strong boost in the run game, and WR Chris Olave and rookie WR Jordan Tyson (when he returns from injury) will provide Shough with enough firepower to make a legitimate leap forward. The defense was actually sneaky good towards the tail end of 2025, look for former first round pick DE Chase Young to continue the success he enjoyed at the position this season (finished 2025 with 10 sacks in his last 12 games).

4

Atlanta Falcons

2025
8-9
Breakout Candidate
DT Brandon Dorlus

Atlanta has a decent roster on paper. A lot of firepower on offense (RB Bijan Robinson, WR Drake London, TE Kyle Pitts), and a defense that looks to be improving a lot over last year with the additions of LBs Azeez Ojulari and Za’Darius Smith. CB AJ Terrell is one of the best cover corners in football, and S Jessie Bates III is a potential All Pro at his position as well. The main problem in Atlanta is the most important part of having a successful football team, which is their Quarterbacking Conundrum. After the shocking first round selection of Washington standout QB Michael Penix Jr a few years back, things haven’t really panned out for the southpaw. The addition of QB Tua Tagovailoa from Miami further muddies the waters for Atlanta. For this team to have any sort of success in 2026, they need to firmly establish their offensive identity, and fast.

1

Los Angeles Rams

2025
12-5
Breakout Candidate
TE Terrance Ferguson

Difficult as it is for me to stomach putting a team that won the Super Bowl at second in their division, HC Sean McVay’s Rams are really the first NFL super team that we have seen constructed in this modern era. One of the best offenses in football last year returns reigning MVP QB Matthew Stafford, OPOTY runner up WR Puka Nacua, WR Davante Adams (14 touchdowns in 2025), and elusive RB Kyren Williams. The offense is elite, but the reason the Rams are so heavily favored to win the Super Bowl in 2026 (5-to-1 odds) are the almost comical additions they’ve made to their already strong defense. DE Myles Garrett, who won DPOTY last year. CBs Jaylen Watson and Trent McDuffie, both former Super Bowl winners from Kansas City. And, of course, the return from retirement of surefire first-ballot Hall of Famer DT Aaron Donald. The Rams are old. I get it. But this is not a team of the future, this is a team of the now, and as far as rosters go, this is the best team in football.

2

Seattle Seahawks

2025
14-3
Breakout Candidate
RB Jadarian Price (R)

Wildly underrated as I see it entering the 2026 season, it feels like nobody is really talking about Seattle for a team that firmly cemented themselves as the NFL’s best team by winning the Super Bowl last season. Everyone is talking about the Rams and their ridiculous additions, and rightfully so, but the Seahawks seem to strangely be flying under the radar for a team of their calibre. QB Sam Darnold led this group to the promised land in 2025 behind a ridiculously efficient and turnover-avoidant scheme, with help from 2025’s OPOTY WR Jaxon Smith-Njigba. WR Rashid Shaheed seems to be making large gains in the offseason as well and should fit in as a more significant part of the offense in 2026. The departure of Super Bowl MVP Kenneth Walker for Kansas City certainly hurts, but the one-two punch of RB Zach Charbonnet (when he returns from injury) and rookie RB Jadarian Price should give Seattle a strong and balanced offense. The defense is elite, led by CB Devon Witherspoon, DT Leonard Williams, and emergent breakout-star candidate DT Byron Murphy. Seattle should absolutely be in contention for a repeat title this year, as it appears the road to glory runs through the best division in football.

3

San Francisco 49ers

2025
12-5
Breakout Candidate
WR De’Zhaun Stribling (R)

For whatever reason, I just don’t really believe in the 49ers this season. HC Kyle Shanahan is a great leader, and this team should absolutely be competitive, but I think their division and conference as a whole is just too difficult to really make any noise come playoff time. QB Brock Purdy returns to lead the offense, coupled with All Pro RB Christian McCaffrey and will lean on the new-look receiving corps of WR Mike Evans and rookie WR De’Zhaun Stribling. The defense gets a big boost with the arrival of new defensive coordinator Raheem Morris, and the return of LB Fred Warner across LB Dre Greenlaw should see this unit towards the top half of overall rankings come season’s end. There’s a lot to be excited about in the Bay Area, but the uphill battle is just too steep to climb this year.

4

Arizona Cardinals

2025
3-14
Breakout Candidate
RB Jeremiyah Love (R)

The Arizona Cardinals are in all-out rebuild mode and should finish towards the bottom of overall league standings in 2026. QB Jacoby Brissett takes the reins as the official starter following a surprisingly efficient 2025 campaign and the departure of franchise stalwart QB Kyler Murray for Minnesota. Third overall pick RB Jeremiyah Love will make an impact early for Arizona, and look for third year WR Marvin Harrison Jr to finally break out now that he has some consistency at the quarterback position. TE Trey McBride is another star piece of this offense, but outside of those names there just isn’t enough for the Cardinals to bank on to really expect any sort of success this year. The defense is plain bad (28th overall last season), and doesn't really have any bright spots outside of veteran S Budda Baker and LB Mack Wilson Jr.

1

Seattle Seahawks

7-2

Seattle is the league’s most complete team through ten weeks, and it hasn’t been particularly close. In a season with questions abound for some of the perennial top contenders, the Seahawks have risen significantly above middling preseason expectations to claim the midseason ranking’s top spot. QB Sam Darnold has been on fire in his new home, boasting the league’s highest QBR and showing strong command of the offense all while protecting the football. Young WR Jaxon Smith-Njigba is a legitimate threat to break Calvin Johnson’s all-time receiving yards record, and has been simply unstoppable this season. The defense is all world, and has absolutely stifled opposing offenses all season, even with a few injuries throughout the course of the year. It will be an exciting finish in the league’s best division to end the year, but Seattle looks like a true contender.

2

Los Angeles Rams

7-2

The other top contender in the NFC West is Sean McVay’s Rams. The Rams are the only other NFL team with no real questions or weaknesses, and they play a true complementary brand of football. The ageless QB Matthew Stafford is quietly having an MVP season, and is leaning on the elite tandem of WRs Puka Nacua and Davante Adams en route to the league-lead for touchdown passes. The offense has been unstoppable lately, and the defense, which looked to be LA’s calling card at the season’s outset, has been as advertised. The opportunistic defense has been making the most of their takeaways, and boasts the league’s fourth-ranked differential in that category. The road to the Super Bowl will go through the NFC West this season, as Seattle and Los Angeles are head and shoulders above the rest of the league at the midpoint.

3

New England Patriots

8-2

The more things change, the more things stay the same. After a half-decade stretch residing in the basement of the AFC, Patriot fans have returned to a land of familiarity at the top of their conference. Strength of scheduling notwithstanding, the Patriots have been soundly defeating the teams in front of them, making up a catalog of wins that includes other contenders in Tampa Bay and Buffalo. HC Mike Vrabel looks like the frontrunner for Coach of the Year, as he has brought the Patriots back to the forefront of the league behind the explosive emergence of second-year signal caller QB Drake Maye. Maye is currently the favorite to win MVP, but the less eye-catching reason for New England’s success is the defense. The Patriots boast the league’s second best scoring defense and have been nothing short of elite against the run.

4

Detroit Lions

6-3

Things look a little different in Detroit this year following the departures of Ben Johnson and Aaron Glenn. Make no mistake though, this team is still elite on both sides of the ball, and when both units are clicking they look unbeatable. QB Jared Goff remains the most effective game manager in football, but that is no knock against his skillset. The explosive playmakers surrounding him make his job much easier, as WR Amon-Ra St. Brown and RB Jahmyr Gibbs are All-Pro level players who play in an effective system and behind an elite offensive line. The defense is fantastic as well when healthy, and the return of EDGE Aidan Hutchinson has bolstered a unit that looked lost towards the tail end of last year’s campaign. Minus a few hiccups thus far, the Lions look poised to once again win the NFC North and it will be up to Dan Campbell to get one of the NFL’s best rosters over the hump.

5

Indianapolis Colts

8-2

Yep, Colts at #5, just like we all predicted during the offseason. This season’s most surprising turnaround has taken the league by storm, and HC Shane Steichen has his squad primed to make a deep postseason run. Obviously, QB Daniel Jones has been surgical this year and currently leads the league in passing yards. We all knew that was going to happen. Right? Jokes aside, Jones has struggled a bit over the last few weeks but overall has been historically effective through the air for Indy. It doesn’t hurt that he has RB Jonathan Taylor next to him either, who has a legitimate chance to win MVP. 14 touchdowns and 1400 all-purpose yards through 10 games speaks for itself. Add in trade deadline acquisition CB Sauce Gardner for the Colts, and the sky is the limit for Indy this year.

6

Philadelphia Eagles

6-2

Philly finished last season with one of the most dominant three-month stretch of football we’ve seen in recent memory en route to the franchise’s second Lombardi Trophy. The reigning, defending Super Bowl champs are still elite, make no mistake about it, but there are certainly questions about the Eagles through eight weeks of football. The offense has seemingly taken a pretty substantial step backwards, with the air attack and the run game not living up to the historical highs of last season. The defense is still strong, but it seems as though the departures of DT Milton Williams and EDGE Josh Sweat were a bit more impactful than initially projected. Luckily for Birds fans, Howie Roseman is still the best GM in football and there are rookies waiting in the wings looking to make the jump to stardom on the defensive side of the ball. Philly may not be an overwhelming favorite to repeat this year, but they will still easily cruise to the division title. The first NFC East repeat champion in two decades!

7

Kansas City Chiefs

5-4

This team is significantly better than their record states, and I think anyone who has watched football over the last decade would agree with that claim. Take away the season-opening losses to the Chargers and Ravens, who faced a Chief team riddled by injury and suspension, and the recent loss to the Buffalo Bills, and Kansas City would undoubtedly sit at the top of the list. I’m not putting much stake in those losses though, as Buffalo surely knows, it is not the games in October that matter when it comes to Kansas City. QB Patrick Mahomes is playing at a level we haven’t seen from the future Hall of Famer in a few years, and DC Steve Spagnuolo’s unit remains exotic and effective. Picking against the Chiefs to make it to the Super Bowl is like losing money at this point, so expect the Chiefs to climb this list as the season draws to a close.

8

Buffalo Bills

6-3

What a strange season in Buffalo. It has been hard to get a read on the league’s top contenders from year’s past this season, and Buffalo remains one of the most apparent enigmas through the halfway point. They beat the Chiefs in a thriller one week, then get decimated the next by an atrocious Miami team. QB Josh Allen is still fantastic, but the lack of support around him is starting to catch up, and Allen can’t do it all by himself every week. The defense has also taken a significant step back which puts even more pressure on the offense. It will be up to HC Sean McDermott to guide his team over the hump and past the rival Chiefs come January, or else Bills fans will likely be asking for some sort of large-scale structural change.

9

Green Bay Packers

5-2-1

Green Bay started the year off looking like 17-0 was not out of the question, after walloping the Lions and Commanders to start the season. Newly acquired EDGE Micah Parsons and QB Jordan Love looked like they were about to take over the league, and the rest of the NFL was officially on notice. Since week 2 though, Green Bay has cooled off pretty considerably. The Packers have seemingly been stricken with the oft-maligned syndrome of playing down to the level of their opponents. In the NFL though, winning football is winning football, and their record speaks for itself. If HC Matt LaFleur can clean some things up, Green Bay should make the postseason and compete into January. Look to their Week 10 matchup with Philadelphia as a true litmus test for both squads.

10

Tampa Bay Buccaneers

6-3

The early season darling Tampa Bay Buccaneers are another potential contender who have cooled off in recent weeks. QB Baker Mayfield began the year playing at an MVP-caliber level, but has regressed slightly from his early season highs, although he has still been playing well with a significantly depleted cast around him. Plenty is still going right down south, and the Bucs have a relatively straightforward path to their sixth-straight division title and a playoff berth. This is still a complete football team, and if Mayfield can get back to playing his hard-nosed brand of football and limiting the turnovers, Tampa Bay will be a force to be reckoned with in the postseason. Rookie WR Emeka Egbuka looks like the clear frontrunner for the Offensive Rookie of the Year Award, the latest in a long line of Ohio State products who have turned heads at the pro level immediately. Mayfield and his Bucs will cruise to the division title by season’s end.

11

Los Angeles Chargers

7-3

The Chargers, who started the season off with a bang, have been tricky to get a read on. QB Justin Herbert is a true franchise guy, and has been seeing more success this season by using his athleticism to pick up key first downs and keep his unit out on the field. The Bolts boast one of the league’s elite defenses, as HC Jim Harbaugh’s mentality centers around a hard-nosed defensive unit and a tendency to want to punch you in the mouth running the ball on offense. The Chargers play in a very difficult division, but should be able to sneak into a Wild Card spot in a conference that is relatively weak outside of their own backyard.

12

Denver Broncos

8-2

Who are the Denver Broncos? That is a question that will certainly be answered in the coming weeks, as Sean Payton’s team looks to ride one of the league’s best defenses into postseason glory. There are no questions on that side of the ball, as EDGE Nik Bonitto and CB Patrick Surtain II are two of the most impactful players in the entire NFL, and the entire unit is about as staunch as it gets against both the run and the pass. The questions for Denver lie on offense, as second year QB Bo Nix has taken a pretty noticeable step back this season. He looks unsure of himself and more reluctant to use his athleticism to make plays, often holding the ball for too long and forcing Denver into difficult conversion scenarios. If Nix can right the ship, Denver should be contending deep into the postseason.

13

Chicago Bears

6-3

Has it finally happened? Have the Bears finally found a quarterback? Through 10 weeks, it certainly appears so. After a rocky start to this year’s campaign, second year QB Caleb Williams looks to be what was promised to Bears fans last year when they selected the USC product with the #1 overall pick in 2024’s draft. The evolution of Caleb Williams is a testament to how much coaching matters for a young quarterback, and now that HC Ben Johnson has taken the reins in the Windy City, the sky appears to be the limit for the Bears. The offense has been fantastic in recent weeks, driven by Williams’ ability to activate the clutch gene and put the team on his back when the stakes are the highest. It may not be Chicago’s year this season with the gauntlet they play in, but the upward trajectory is clear.

14

Baltimore Ravens

4-5

1-5 who? Baltimore has seemingly shrugged off an atrocious, injury-marred start to the year by winning three straight. Luckily for the Ravens, they keep getting healthier, and the other members of the AFC North are looking worse and worse week over week. The biggest injection into this team has been the return of QB Lamar Jackson, who when healthy makes a case year in and year out to be the league’s best player. RB Derrick Henry is starting to slow down, as the future Hall of Famer has eclipsed the 30-year old mark and it is now more imperative than ever that Lamar needs to be truly Lamarvelous. The defense also received a shot in the arm with the return of All Pro safety Kyle Hamilton, and this team looks to be trending at the right time.

15

San Francisco 49ers

6-3

Tough to get a read on the Niners this year. Yet again, San Francisco has been bitten hard by the injury bug, and it warrants the conversation at this point if the team needs a significant overhaul in terms of the training staff department. Backup QB Mac Jones has filled in admirably this year for Brock Purdy, but at this point, the injuries feel like too much to overcome. The loss of LB Fred Warner really cripples the spirit of this team, and although RB Christian McCaffrey has been playing at an elite level this year, there is simply too much competition within the division and the conference to predict a playoff appearance for San Francisco.

16

Pittsburgh Steelers

5-4

Another year in Pittsburgh, and another 9-8 season seems inevitable. QB Aaron Rodgers looked like he had finally found the correct dosage of psychedelics to perform at his 2021 level earlier in the year, but it appears that age has finally caught up to the legendary signal caller. The offense has been decent, but the real problem for Pittsburgh lies on defense. When you’re spending that much money on defense, you need a unit that gets it done, and Pittsburgh’s D has been pretty sub-par through 10 weeks. Factor in the resurgence of the Ravens and a few new faces in the AFC playoff picture, and Pittsburgh is in real danger of missing the playoffs after a hot start to the season.

17

Houston Texans

4-5

Once you hit the back half of the power rankings, team quality seems to tank. Despite the parity in the league being at an all-time high, win-loss records have been pretty lopsided this season, with only a handful of teams hovering around the .500 mark. Houston is one of these teams, and they have started looking better lately after their 0-3 start. The strength of the Texans lies on defense, where they boast the strongest unit in the NFL statistically. The problem for Houston is the continued regression of QB CJ Stroud and the inability of the offense to move the ball efficiently and convert scoring chances into points. Even worse for their outlook, backup QB Davis Mills filled in for Stroud last week, and Houston had their best offensive performance of the season. Not ideal vibes in HTX.

18

Jacksonville Jaguars

5-4

Jacksonville started the year off burning hot, riding a historically opportunistic defense to victories by capitalizing off turnovers. Things have slowed down in a big way lately, and the biggest culprit behind the team’s lack of success is QB Trevor Lawrence. Lawrence has never grown into the elite quarterback that was promised to Jags fans 5 years ago, and his performance throughout his career is holding the team back. Regardless of how hot the defense was at the beginning of the year, that style of winning football games was never going to be sustainable, and the offense needed to explode in terms of productivity to give fans any semblance of stability for the rest of the year. That production has been nowhere to be seen, and the Jags look like a team that will finish around .500 and miss the postseason.

19

Atlanta Falcons

3-6

Disappointing, to say the least for Falcons fans. In what was supposed to be a breakout year for QB Michael Penix Jr, the team has yet to find an identity on offense. This team is much better than their record states, but 3-6 is 3-6. The offense should be extremely explosive on paper, but they haven’t been able to string success along for longer than four quarters. RB Bijan Robinson is probably the most electric player playing on Sundays, but the play calling and lack of offensive rhythm is not doing him justice and severely knee-capping his potential. The defense has surprisingly been elite, with the fourth best EPA against so far this season, but the team has been unable to play complementary football and the Win-Loss column reflects that.

20

Dallas Cowboys

3-4-1

If the offense isn’t breaking records, this team simply has no chance to win. With defensive statistics trailing only the free falling Cincinnati Bengals, there is very little hope that Dallas has any chance to make the postseason this year or in the future. The old adage holds true, and defense not only wins championships but also wins games at this level (see: Denver Broncos), and with the offense falling back to earth in recent weeks, things are looking bleak in Jerryworld. The offseason gamble to deal all-world EDGE Micah Parsons to Green Bay has resulted in the second-worst defense in pro football, and GM/Owner Jerry Jones is finally looking like he is in over his head after a legendary tenure helming America’s Team. QB Dak Prescott has been fantastic this season, pairing especially well with the addition of WR George Pickens. RB Javonte Williams has been excellent as well, but the Cowboys stand no chance without a defense.

21

Carolina Panthers

5-5

We will have to see how the rest of this season shakes out for Carolina. If things continue to go south, as they have been lately, the front office will need to take a long, hard look at the investment they’ve made in continuing to develop QB Bryce Young. I’m a fan of the Alabama product because he seems like an exemplary individual and a great hang, but at this point, it seems like he does not have what it takes to be a winning starting quarterback in this league. This year has been particularly disappointing for Young because of the strides Carolina has made on defense. After being the worst unit in football a year ago, their defense has figured things out a bit and looks like the only thing they are missing in Charlotte is a quarterback. Unless Young can pull a rabbit out a hat to finish the season and sneak into a Wild Card spot, it is likely the end of the road for the former #1 overall pick.

22

Minnesota Vikings

4-5

The Vikings have taken an expected step back this season behind the helm of QB JJ McCarthy. McCarthy, who was Minnesota’s first round pick last year, did not play in 2024, so significant growing pains were to be expected. There is reason to be hopeful in Minnesota, as fans can likely count on HC Kevin O’Connell, one of the league’s best, to be able to mold McCarthy into a winning QB. The defense has been middling, and key departures on that side of the ball have made the Vikings too reliant on heroics from what is essentially a rookie quarterback. More troubling, All Pro WR Justin Jefferson has had his least productive season yet and has been a virtual no-show in games where McCarthy starts. It will be a few years before Minnesota finds themselves relevant again, and that is contingent on the development of their second-year signal caller.

23

Arizona Cardinals

3-6

The Arizona Cardinals seem to have finally moved on from QB Kyler Murray. With the insertion of veteran QB Jacoby Brissett proving to be a better move for the team, and the inability of Murray to really establish any sort of stability in his career outside of a stellar rookie season, it appears a new day is dawning in Phoenix. The team has been a dumpster fire this season, losing multiple winnable games and getting blown out in others. The season is over at the midway point for an Arizona team that some thought may be a dark horse contender, but moving on from Murray is the right move and will allow for Arizona’s front office to develop a different young quarterback for their imminent rebuild.

24

Cincinnati Bengals

3-6

The season was over pretty much as soon as it started, with star QB Joe Burrow going down in Week 2 with yet another catastrophic injury. Veteran QB Joe Flacco has filled in admirably in Burrow’s stead, but even the heroics of a 41-year old aberration cannot make up for the disaster that is the Bengals’ defense. The NFL’s worst defensive unit is pretty much ranked 32nd in every meaningful category, and it continues a worrisome trend over the last few years that despite the firepower on offense, Cincinnati will never be a real contender unless they address the gaping holes on defense. Burrow may return this season, but it doesn’t seem like a move that would make sense given how far outside the playoff picture they look. It appears the Super Bowl appearance hangover continues, and it is high time the Bengals move on from HC Zac Taylor.

25

Miami Dolphins

3-7

An absolutely pitiful start to the season has been unsuccessfully mitigated by the Dolphins, continuing to play losing football even with the increased quality of play from QB Tua Tagovailoa. HC Mike McDaniel’s tenure appears to be over, despite a confounding, dominant win over the Bills recently. The departure of key defensive fixtures have identified the Dolphins as sellers moving into the offseason, and it appears as a full overhaul is in the works for Miami. Lone bright spot RB De’Von Achane has been terrific this season, and could be an attractive trade target if the Dolphins do decide to clean house. That 70-30 win over the Broncos a few years ago seems further away than the Fins’ last Super Bowl appearance (1985).

26

Cleveland Browns

2-7

As miserable as the offense has been this season, there does seem to be hope in Cleveland. The defense is the strong point of this team, and a strong point it certainly is, ranking top 5 in every meaningful defensive statistic. EDGE Myles Garrett continues on his Hall of Fame trajectory, providing a spark for a defense that plays fast and rallies to the ball better than most units in the league. Unfortunately, the elite status of the defense is not enough to win football games when the offense is so historically anemic, and there are no clear answers at the quarterback position. Both QB selections in the 2024 draft for Cleveland appear to be whiffs, with QB Dillon Gabriel getting handed the reins after the departure of QB Joe Flacco to Cincinnati, and playing poorly, as is to be expected. The only hope for Cleveland’s offense is to somehow hit a home run in the 2027 draft, which should be loaded with quarterback talent.

27

Washington Commanders

3-7

Oof. What a fall from grace for the NFC runner ups. Washington has been mauled by injury this year, but even that fact should not take away from the fact that the product on the field in the nation’s capital right now is historically awful. Reigning Offensive Rookie of the Year QB Jayden Daniels has spent most of this year on the sideline, and another grotesque injury in Week 8 against Seattle should end his season. Washington’s front office simply put too much faith in the rookie, and refused to fill any of the roster’s needs with young, developmental prospects and instead opted for bringing in even more talent that was great in 2018, but old and ineffective in 2025. HC Dan Quinn should be fired at season’s end, as the current stretch of Commanders football is one of the worst in history, with blow out losses (21+) in four straight games, good for the worst mark since the 2002 Cardinals.

28

Las Vegas Raiders

2-7

The offseason dice roll that the Raiders made for veteran QB Geno Smith has not paid off. Smith has been one of the league’s worst quarterbacks, and the success of the team has followed suit. Not much to be said for Vegas outside of the encouraging rookie year of RB Ashton Jeanty and the continued dominance of EDGE Maxx Crosby. Jeanty has been effective, but a rookie running back cannot solely provide the spark needed to consistently win NFL football games. TE Brock Bowers is still elite, but his season has been limited with injuries. Until they invest a high draft pick in an elite quarterback prospect, the Raiders will continue to be the bottom-feeders of the meat grinder that is the AFC West.

29

New York Giants

2-8

Hope has arrived in New Jersey! Although, in typical Giant fashion, that hope was ripped from the waiting hands of fans as soon as it appeared. The Giants’ 2025 draft class looked excellent early, as the combination of RB Cam Skattebo and QB Jaxson Dart is going to be electric for years to come. Unfortunately, Skattebo suffered a season-ending leg injury and Dart is out indefinitely following a concussion last week. Luckily, #3 overall pickup EDGE Abdul Carter is playing as advertised, and helps support an elite defensive front alongside veterans DT Dexter Lawrence, EDGE Brian Burns, and EDGE Kayvon Thibodeaux. This year is not the Giants’ year, clearly, but there is hope on the horizon for the G-Men.

30

New Orleans Saints

2-8

QB Spencer Rattler was serviceable through the first nine games, but the lack of winning paved the way for second round pick QB Tyler Shough to take over the starting role. Shough is 1-0 in his career, and led the Saints to victory last week against the Panthers. One feel-good win aside, the Saints are a rebuilding team that will not see success in the next 3 years. The defense has holes, the offense is inexperienced, and the coaching staff has been largely ineffective. Another year at the bottom of the NFC South for the Saints.

New York Jets

The biggest story with the Jets is that they have officially entered total shut down mode after dealing CB Sauce Gardner and DT Quinnen Williams at the deadline, arguably the team’s two best players. 32

Tennessee Titans

A tough start to the beginning of QB Cam Ward’s career in 2025. The Titans are inept organizationally, and are awful on both sides of the ball. Ward is relatively innocent, as a terrible defense that can’t stop a nose bleed and a geriatric supporting cast on offense do not make a strong developmental foundation for a young quarterback. There are basically no positives this season for Tennessee, and they will likely finish the year 1-16 with the #1 overall pick in next year’s draft.

The addition of Cincinnati RB Joe Mixon has bolstered the Houston rushing attack, and gives them a solid dual-threat attack when moving the football. The reason behind the Texans' 6-3 start, however, has been the defense. Their unit ranks #2 in yards allowed per game, and the pass rush duo of Will Anderson Jr. and Danielle Hunter have been making life difficult for opposing quarterbacks. Former first round pick CB Derek Stingley Jr. is coming on nicely this season as well, and Houston should cruise to an AFC South title.

11

Atlanta Falcons

6-3

Atlanta has had a surprising start to the 2024 season. After signing veteran quarterback Kirk Cousins from Minnesota, the Falcons have finally utilized their recent first round draft picks on offense to turn into one of the league's premier scoring threats. RB Bijan Robinson and TE Kyle Pitts are having breakout seasons, and the addition of Chicago WR Darnell Mooney is allowing Cousins to flourish in the A. Cousins has been balling out, as evidenced by a league-high 509 yard passing performance earlier this year, and his offensive stewardship has Atlanta in the driver's seat in the NFC South. The defense, which has been suspect in past seasons, has been gelling this year behind the solid play of former All Pro cornerback AJ Terrell. Atlanta has been one of the league's best surprises this season.

12

Cincinnati Bengals

4-6

After a typical Cincinnati start, the Bengals look to be figuring things out again. QB Joe Burrow has been playing at a truly elite level, and is probably playing the best football of his career since getting drafted in 2020. The defense, which looked absolutely abysmal at the beginning of the season, has started to gel, at least to the point where Burrow doesn't need to score on every single drive to keep them in the game. WR Ja'Marr Chase is the league's most dangerous deep threat and gives Cincinnati a chance to score on every single offense snap. The loss of RB Joe Mixon has been crucial for the Bengals' rushing attack, but second year RB Chase Brown has been very solid as a replacement and gives them a multifaceted offensive attack. If the Bengals can continue their hot streak as of late, they could make a legitimate push in one of the best divisions in football. A close loss to Baltimore in Week 10 does not sway my opinion on the Bengals being one of the better teams in the league.

13

Tampa Bay Buccaneers

4-5

The story of Tampa Bay this season has been the continued excellence of 6th-year signal caller Baker Mayfield. Mayfield has once again defied the odds and has Tampa Bay's offense firing on all cylinders, even without his two best receivers in future Hall of Famer Mike Evans and veteran Chris Godwin. Mayfield has lit up virtually every defense he has faced up to this point, although he does need to limit his takeaways moving forward. A key facet of the Bucs' success on offense this season has been their surprising ability to run the football. Rookie RB Bucky Irving has been shining so far this season behind Tampa Bay's excellent offensive line, spearheaded by superstar LT Tristan Wirfs. Wirfs has quietly been one of the NFL's best players and is a key reason why Tampa Bay is in the mix for the NFC South title. They may not have a winning record at this point, but their ability to take Kansas City to the wire in primetime last week shows this is a legit squad that could make noise in a stacked NFC.

14

Los Angeles Chargers

5-4

With the hiring of former Michigan Head Coach Jim Harbaugh, this team is finally moving in the right direction. Offseason darling QB Justin Herbert has continued to be a very solid commander of this offense, but he now has a legitimate team around him. The defense has been one of the league's best through nine weeks, holding teams to a league-best 12.6 points/game and just a hair over 300 yards/game, good for eighth in the NFL. DE Khalil Mack is having a renaissance season, and when his counterpart DE Joey Bosa gets back to full strength this defense should look even scarier. Although they may not win their division, I fully expect the Chargers to be in the mix come playoff time and may even play spoiler to their archrivals in Kansas City.

15

Los Angeles Rams

4-4

After a brutal 1-4 start, the Rams are beginning to look dangerous again. QB Matthew Stafford continues to show why he is one of the league's most underrated signal callers, and with the emergence of veteran receiver DeMarcus Robinson and the return of superstar receiver Cooper Kupp, the Rams look poised for an NFC West crown and an interesting playoff run. The defense looked shaky to start, after losing future Hall of Famer Aaron Donald on the interior, but Los Angeles' ability to draft well at this position over the last two seasons is only making them better. Rookie DE Jared Verse is the early favorite for Defensive Rookie of the Year, and the addition of safety Kam Curl has this defense looking stingy as of late. Head Coach Sean McVay is one of the best in the league for a reason, and the Rams should be in a dogfight with Seattle and San Francisco come December.

16

Denver Broncos

5-4

The Broncos are another club that bet on a young quarterback early in the draft, and this gamble has appeared to be paying off for them thus far. Oregon QB Bo Nix has had his up and downs through nine weeks, but has recently been showing why Denver took the chance on him by limiting turnovers and showing legitimate toughness running the football. Veteran WR Courtland Sutton is developing a strong chemistry with Nix, and they have recently been doing a much better job of running the football. The real story in Denver lies on the other side of the ball, however. The Broncos' defense has been elite so far this year, and CB Patrick Surtain II is a big reason why this team is off to a surprising start, and has a chance for a berth in the AFC playoffs. Denver's defense is currently holding teams to 17.9 points/game, good for third in the league, and this is the main reason they find themselves in the top half of the rankings.

17

Seattle Seahawks

4-5

Seattle has been a bit of an enigma this season. Following a 3-0 start, the Seahawks have regressed on both sides of the ball. Veteran journeyman QB Geno Smith is one of the league's true gunslingers, and his boom-or-bust play style has both won and lost games for Seattle this season. Superstar WR DK Metcalf's absence has also been a significant blow to the offense, as a knee sprain has limited their offense's ability to crack big plays, as Metcalf was among the league's premier deep threats before his injury. After a brilliant start from the defensive unit, injuries have severely impacted their ability to make stops, although rookie DT Byron Murphy Jr. has shown up for Seattle as one of the league's premier young interior defensive linemen. Second year corner Devon Witherspoon continues to be one of the most exciting defensive players to watch in the NFC, and expect one of the league's youngest teams to evolve into a legitimate threat in the next few seasons.

San Francisco 49ers

4-4

A team that should climb on this list as the season continues, the 49ers have gone from a top three team in the league last season to an average- to- mediocre squad. An injury-riddled roster and regression from their young quarterback Brock Purdy are mostly to blame for their sluggish start after a loss in the Super Bowl last season. Reigning Offensive Player of the Year Christian McCaffrey has still yet to take a snap in San Francisco's backfield, and although RB Jordan Mason has been carrying the torch well in CMC's absence, the team seems to be missing the spark that has propelled them deep into the postseason recently. The defense remains a top 10 unit, and the 49ers should continue to move up this list if Shanahan can figure things out offensively.

19

Indianapolis Colts

4-5

Arguably ranked too high in this list, Indianapolis has been another very strange team this season. One week they look solid, and the next week they look hapless. Most of the blame for their average start this year can be placed on the shoulders of second year quarterback Anthony Richardson. Richardson can dazzle at times, but is statistically one of the worst quarterbacks of the last 30 years in terms of quarterback rating and completion percentage in NFL history. His raw athleticism and bruising running style strikes fear in opposing defenses but also has led to numerous injuries over his career and this season is no different. Following his benching, the Colts have inserted veteran QB Joe Flacco, and the offense looks like more of the same following the switch. The Colts looks like no threat in the AFC, but could vie for a wild card berth if things fall their way.

Chicago Bears

4-4

There are few fates in life I can imagine that are as cruel as being a Bears fan. All of this depended on the speed of Williams' acclimatization to the pros, and the results have been, well, mixed. After a 4-2 start where Williams had started to look elite, Chicago has crashed back down to earth, following a gut-wrenching loss on a hail mary and a blowout in back to back weeks. The team appears to have quit on Head Coach Matt Eberflus, and the calls for his firing from the Chicago faithful are deafening. The bright spot so far has been the defense, which looked like the NFL's most stalwart unit through the first seven weeks of the season but has regressed recently. Expect the Bears to finish last in the league's best division.

Arizona Cardinals

5-4

The Cardinals have probably been the toughest team for me to pin down thus far. Murray is currently 5th in the NFL in QBR and that number has been bolstered this season following the addition of Ohio State WR Marvin Harrison Jr. The question with this team lies in the defense, which has not looked good this year and is among the league's worst statistically. The Cardinals currently hold the lead in the wide open NFC West, but I just do not trust this defense to take this team to playoffs, even if Murray continues to turn heads with his dual-threat abilities.

Dallas Cowboys

I'd like to state my bias early in this one… nothing has made me happier this season than the implosion of the Dallas Cowboys. The defense has also been alarmingly mediocre this season, as the absence of superstar linebacker Micah Parsons has severely crippled this unit and the Cowboys look to be in freefall over the last few weeks, losing their last three contests. Expect Head Coach Mike McCarthy to get the axe if things continue to go the way they've been going in JerryWorld.

New York Jets

3-6

The Jets have been, in my opinion, the league's most disappointing team so far this season. It's not all bad in the Big Apple, though, as second year receiver Garrett Wilson is emerging into a legitimate superstar and Rodgers' favorite target, which is a testament to his skill as Rodgers' former teammates Allen Lazard and Davante Adams are catching passes for New York as well.
