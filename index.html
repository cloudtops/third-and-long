Bake or Shake · Weeks 1–3

Bake or Shake

The Gospel According to Baker Mayfield

By Nathanael Kjormoe

Weekly Rundown / Power Rankings

After Week 6

Well, there you have it, folks. Another topsy-turvy weekend of NFL football. Things starting to look a little clearer for you yet after six weeks of games? Me neither. Some serious movement going on in these power rankings this week, but I am starting to get a better idea of who most of these teams actually are. A lot of the initial mirages, either for better or worse, are in fact beginning to subside. All that being said, a +.500 correct rate in my Pick ’Em league still eludes me. So take everything I’m saying with a few tablespoons of salt. Without further ado, let’s talk a little ball.

If you’d like to read my full thoughts on Thursday’s matchup between Seattle and Denver, check it out here. If you don’t, that’s fine. Just know that every time you don’t click on one of our hyperlinks to a different article, a puppy dies. If you’d like to read my full thoughts on what was my Game of the Week for Week 6, click here. A nice coincidence for me that the first two games of the week are already checked off in full article form. That’s the 3rd & Long difference! Anyway, we’ll kick it off with Bears/Falcons. A thunderous return for QB Caleb Williams, who set a new career watermark for single game completion percentage. Chicago burst out to a 31-7 lead before taking their foot off the gas, although they successfully thwarted Atlanta’s comeback attempt. Chicago rolled to a 31-23 victory and improved to 4-2 on the year, and the sky is the limit for this team with their golden boy back under center. In a game that very nearly put me to sleep, Cleveland defeated Baltimore 13-10. QB Lamar Jackson did not play, still inactive from a nagging ankle injury. Do you really want me to recap this? Please don’t make me. The Browns won and improved to 4-2, although the offense is still awful. Defense is great. That’s all you’re gonna get from me on this one.

We interrupt this program to bring you breaking news from the city of Indianapolis, Indiana. The 2026 Tennessee Titans have won a football game. I repeat, the 2026 Tennessee Titans have won a football game! Cam Ward haters, where you at now? Oh, he only threw for 102 yards? The Titans only won this game on the strength of their defense and special teams? Okay, yeah, fair. But wins are the only quarterback stat that matters, baby! Colts fans, time to get the paper bags out? Off we go to the northeast, where the New England Patriots defeated the New York Jets in a game that looked close but didn’t really feel close. Drake Maye is now officially revived. One week is a fluke, two weeks is an eye raise, three weeks is a trend. Dude is back. Someone who is also back, but not back at the same time is WR AJ Brown. Not a great showing for the superstar receiver in his first game returning from injury. Want a real superstar receiver? Look across the sideline to Jets’ rookie WR Omar Cooper Jr. This guy was special last year at Indiana and looks to continue that trend in the pros after returning from his own injury. Anyway, the Patriots are good, the Jets are bad, and water is wet.

Tyler Shough and the Saints defeated the Giants, propelled to victory by the right arm of Shough and the re-animated corpse of RB Alvin Kamara. Kamara is probably a Hall of Famer, right? Hall of Very Good, at the very least. The Saints jumped out to a 34-10 victory before pulling their starters and allowing for QB JJ McCarthy to nearly engineer a comeback of his own. No, not a career comeback, just a this-game comeback. I have no hope for you, Nine. The Giants are probably the worst football team with a winning record I have ever seen through six weeks. Yes, I’m still salty about last week. At least I’m honest about it.

In the City of Brotherly Love, QB Bryce Young continued his incendiary pace en route to a 25-16 Carolina victory. Instead of lambasting the performance that the Eagles gave today, I just want to mention that I feel absolutely awful for Jalen Hurts. His is a very scary situation, and I speak for all of us at 3rd & Long in wishing him a very safe and speedy recovery. Guys like Hurts make the NFL a better product for everyone, and he seems like an exemplary human being in addition to being a truly great quarterback. I can’t tell you how many times I’ve thrown a shoe at my TV after the Commanders go down four scores on another Hurts tush push. But it’s those emotions, negative or positive, that make football such a special thing for so many of us. It’s the reason why we even cheer at all for our hapless clubs, and the reason that when our teams do win, victory feels so sweet. Can’t wait to see you back out there again as soon as you’re all good, Jalen, football will truly be better for it. The Panthers improved to 3-2, and the Eagles fell to 2-4.

Down to sunny Tampa, where the Pittsburgh Steelers completed a furious comeback in overtime against the red-hot (?) Buccaneers. Steeler QB Aaron Rodgers continues to oscillate rapidly between looking like a 75-year-old and looking like it’s 2011 again. It shouldn’t have to be said at this point, but if you mock Rodgers’ signature celebration after sacking him, your team is going to get torched. Pittsburgh rallied from a 16-3 deficit and won the game, courtesy of a touchdown pass from Rodgers to rookie WR Germie Bernard, who was everywhere today. In the biggest upset of the week, Arizona defeated the Los Angeles Rams on a last second field goal from K Chad Ryland. There’s no easy way to say this. The Rams are in hell. Everyone who gave the Rams the August Lombardi are looking pretty silly now, as the preseason Super Bowl favorites sit at 2-4. The funniest part is that most of their losses have come due to special teams errors, which is so silly and such an avoidable thing. McVay will likely have his club rolling again upon the return of reigning Defensive Player of the Year Myles Garrett and a favorable upcoming schedule, but man. Having your punter kicking PAT’s is a bad look. Flowers for the Cardinal defense, which actually looked elite today after getting thoroughly toasted throughout the year. First year head coach Mike LaFleur completed the SoFi Sweep, defeating both Los Angeles teams on the road in one season.

Chiefs vs. Chargers was a snoozefest. Not a ton for you guys on this one that you don’t already know. Justin Herbert is a bad quarterback, and the Chiefs are a Super Bowl frontrunner. It’s so infuriating watching Herbert evade three sacks on one play and promptly throw the football directly to a linebacker with no receiver within 15 yards of him. The Chargers are one of two winless teams remaining, and as such fall to #32 in the power rankings. This team might be worse than those Andy Reid/Pat Mahomes State Farm commercials. Just gouge my eyes out already. The Bills and Raiders played in the runner-up contender for Game of the Week, a shootout in the desert that was highlighted by a Josh Allen-esque touchdown pass from, well, Josh Allen. Raiders QB Kirk Cousins is elite again, and was incredibly efficient doling out passes to the best tight end tandem in the league, TE Brock Bowers and TE Michael Mayer. The Bills are a Super Bowl contender, but they go as Allen goes. Allen is Sisyphus, but the Buffalo defense is the boulder.

Sunday night was largely unexciting, unless you’re a Packers fan, in which case you were likely ecstatic with the play of your defense and the return to form of QB Jordan Love. Or if you, like me, just hate the Cowboys and watching them get embarrassed in primetime is almost better than your own team winning. The “Greatest Offense There Is” put up seven points in this game when it mattered, and QB Dak Prescott looked lost against the blitz. Never thought I would write that sentence. Dallas, like the Rams, is also in hell. The Cowboys should probably fire coach Brian Schottenheimer at the bye, but far be it from Jerry to admit that he was wrong. 6-11 incoming. Green Bay looks like the Green Bay that we expected coming into the season, and will only get better when Great Guy and All Around Family Man RB Josh Jacobs returns next week. Love is playing at an elite level, the defense is stifling, and the WR duo of Christian Watson and Matthew Golden looks like one of the most electric in the league.

We closed out the week in the Bay Area, where San Francisco jumped out to a 20-3 lead in the first half and outgained the clearly outmatched Commanders by 213 yards. After WR Mike Evans went down the Niner offense stalled, and Washington remembered that they have a perfect, infallible, incredible unicorn of a quarterback and dominated the 49ers throughout the entire second half. Not enough to get the W, as Dan Quinn once again tried his absolutely hardest to lose this game, but at least QB Jayden Daniels looked great for a second week in a row. It’s so depressing as a Commander fan to see who I think is a Top 5 quarterback in this league paired with a Bottom 5 roster and coaching staff. I know you probably want me to mention him throwing the ball away on fourth down in the first half, but I’m not going to. I think he saw the flag on the ground and was trying to do his best Aaron Rodgers free play impression. Sending hugs to my guy WR Terry McLaurin who was carted off early in this one, I’m hoping against hope that last night wasn’t his final time suiting up in the burgundy and gold. Anyway, San Francisco improves to 6-0, and Washington falls to a disappointing 1-4-1.

Alrighty, there ya go! All my reads on all the games from this week. Looking ahead to Week 7, we have some absolute classics on the docket. Browns/Titans, Jets/Dolphins, Cowboys/Eagles. What’s not to love? See you then!
