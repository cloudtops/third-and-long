North #2 · 2025 12-5 · Breakout: DT Kentavius Street

In 2025, QB Caleb Williams proved himself as a player worthy of the first overall pick in 2024 and then some. Williams provided Chicago with playmaking ability at the quarterback position that the NFL’s second-oldest franchise has honestly never seen in its history. Williams led Chicago to a jaw-dropping postseason comeback over the hated Packers, and had Bears fans partying like it was 1985. The Bears’ success largely runs through if Williams will be able to duplicate and build on the gains he made in HC Ben Johnson’s offensive system last year. Holes in the defense will make this more difficult for Caleb and the Bears, but I am drinking the Kool-Aid and I predict Chicago will return to the postseason in 2026.

2025 · Midseason

Ranked #13 · 6-3

Has it finally happened? Have the Bears finally found a quarterback? Through 10 weeks, it certainly appears so. After a rocky start to this year’s campaign, second year QB Caleb Williams looks to be what was promised to Bears fans last year when they selected the USC product with the #1 overall pick in 2024’s draft. The evolution of Caleb Williams is a testament to how much coaching matters for a young quarterback, and now that HC Ben Johnson has taken the reins in the Windy City, the sky appears to be the limit for the Bears. The offense has been fantastic in recent weeks, driven by Williams’ ability to activate the clutch gene and put the team on his back when the stakes are the highest. It may not be Chicago’s year this season with the gauntlet they play in, but the upward trajectory is clear.

North #3 · 2024 5-12 · Breakout: QB Caleb Williams

The Detroit Lions’ loss is the Chicago Bears’ gain. Newly hired HC Ben Johnson will shake things up considerably in the Windy City, and second year QB Caleb Williams will show strong improvement, building on a rookie season that had some pretty high highs with some fairly low lows. Bears fans are presumably ready to get hurt again, after the buzz out of Bears camp is sounding better and better by the day. Rookie WR Luther Burden III will complement WR Rome Odunze nicely, but the biggest addition to the offense will be rookie TE Colston Loveland, who was an absolute monster throughout his collegiate career at Michigan. It’s not playoff time just yet in Chicago, but they will certainly be a team to watch out for in the next few years.

2024 · Midseason

Ranked #20 · 4-4

There are few fates in life I can imagine that are as cruel as being a Bears fan. After drafting "generational" USC QB Caleb Williams with the first overall pick of the draft, and adding WR Keenan Allen, the Bears had the splashiest offseason of any team and looked to be in a position to make a playoff run. All of this depended on the speed of Williams' acclimatization to the pros, and the results have been, well, mixed. After a 4-2 start where Williams had started to look elite, Chicago has crashed back down to earth, following a gut-wrenching loss on a hail mary and a blowout in back to back weeks. The team appears to have quit on Head Coach Matt Eberflus, and the calls for his firing from the Chicago faithful are deafening. The bright spot so far has been the defense, which looked like the NFL's most stalwart unit through the first seven weeks of the season but has regressed recently. Expect the Bears to finish last in the league's best division.
