2026 · Preseason

North #1 · 2025 6-11 · Breakout: DE Boye Mafe

Have the Bengals become the new Cowboys? Despite missing out on postseason festivities each year since 2022, the Who Dey faithful remain stalwart in their optimism season in and season out. Is it the Bengal’s year? Maybe. The here and now may be the best they’ve looked in the preseason since 2022, as there are few questions about their elite offense and ability to produce fireworks. QB Joe Burrow returns from a nasty turf toe injury that sidelined him for a significant portion of 2025, and WR’s Ja’Marr Chase and Tee Higgins look to capitalize on playing a full campaign with their generational signal caller. The main reason for the Bengals success this season will come from plugging defensive holes with the additions of DT Dexter Lawrence, DT Jonathan Allen, and DE Boye Mafe.

2025 · Midseason

Ranked #24 · 3-6

The season was over pretty much as soon as it started, with star QB Joe Burrow going down in Week 2 with yet another catastrophic injury. Veteran QB Joe Flacco has filled in admirably in Burrow’s stead, but even the heroics of a 41-year old aberration cannot make up for the disaster that is the Bengals’ defense. The NFL’s worst defensive unit is pretty much ranked 32nd in every meaningful category, and it continues a worrisome trend over the last few years that despite the firepower on offense, Cincinnati will never be a real contender unless they address the gaping holes on defense. Burrow may return this season, but it doesn’t seem like a move that would make sense given how far outside the playoff picture they look. It appears the Super Bowl appearance hangover continues, and it is high time the Bengals move on from HC Zac Taylor.

2025 · Preseason

North #2 · 2024 9-8 · Breakout: DE Myles Murphy

After an MVP-level performance from QB Joe Burrow last season, the Bengals return to 2025 with arguably the league’s best offense after resigning WRs Ja’Marr Chase and WR Tee Higgins. The big question for Cincy this year lies on the other side of the ball, where their defense hung the team out to dry time and time again last year, relegating one of the most exciting teams in football to their couches last January. It remains to be seen whether or not the Bengals’ front office did enough in the offseason to address their defense, and if their new-look unit can just be average, Cincinnati should be able to make it to the postseason as a wild card team.

2024 · Midseason

Ranked #12 · 4-6

After a typical Cincinnati start, the Bengals look to be figuring things out again. QB Joe Burrow has been playing at a truly elite level, and is probably playing the best football of his career since getting drafted in 2020. The defense, which looked absolutely abysmal at the beginning of the season, has started to gel, at least to the point where Burrow doesn't need to score on every single drive to keep them in the game. WR Ja'Marr Chase is the league's most dangerous deep threat and gives Cincinnati a chance to score on every single offense snap. The loss of RB Joe Mixon has been crucial for the Bengals' rushing attack, but second year RB Chase Brown has been very solid as a replacement and gives them a multifaceted offensive attack. If the Bengals can continue their hot streak as of late, they could make a legitimate push in one of the best divisions in football. A close loss to Baltimore in Week 10 does not sway my opinion on the Bengals being one of the better teams in the league.
