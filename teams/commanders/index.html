2026 · Preseason

East #4 · 2025 5-12 · Breakout: WR Antonio Williams (R)

Sigh. After a disaster of a season for Washington in 2025, a campaign marred by injuries all over both sides of the ball, the road back to success just looks more difficult in 2026. QB Jayden Daniels returns to lead the offense alongside WRs Terry McLaurin and newly acquired Stefon Diggs. RB Ja’Cory Croskey-Merritt and RB Rachaad White will take some pressure off Daniels in the run game, and barring any more catastrophic injuries (T Laremy Tunsil is already lost for the season), this offense should replicate some of the success of Daniels’ stellar rookie campaign in 2024. The defense, which was atrocious last year, has plugged some holes in the acquisitions of DE Odafe Oweh and rookie LB Sonny Styles. This unit should likely be better than 2025, again, barring the bite from the injury bug that the team faced in 2025. The problem for Washington isn’t necessarily the roster, it is the scheduling start to the year that puts the Commanders in an admittedly terrible position, playing a multitude of road games against some of the league’s best teams.

2025 · Midseason

Ranked #27 · 3-7

Oof. What a fall from grace for the NFC runner ups. Washington has been mauled by injury this year, but even that fact should not take away from the fact that the product on the field in the nation’s capital right now is historically awful. Reigning Offensive Rookie of the Year QB Jayden Daniels has spent most of this year on the sideline, and another grotesque injury in Week 8 against Seattle should end his season. Washington’s front office simply put too much faith in the rookie, and refused to fill any of the roster’s needs with young, developmental prospects and instead opted for bringing in even more talent that was great in 2018, but old and ineffective in 2025. HC Dan Quinn should be fired at season’s end, as the current stretch of Commanders football is one of the worst in history, with blow out losses (21+) in four straight games, good for the worst mark since the 2002 Cardinals.

2025 · Preseason

East #2 · 2024 12-5 · Breakout: RB Bill Croskey-Merritt (R)

The NFL’s biggest surprise last season is ready to defy expectations again this year. Second year QB Jayden Daniels is coming off of the greatest rookie quarterback season we’ve ever seen in a campaign that saw the Commanders go all the way from 4-13 in 2023, to the NFC Championship in 2024. Last year surpassed even Commanders’ fans' wildest dreams, but it doesn’t look like it was one miracle fluke season for the Burgundy and Gold. All-Pro WR Terry McLaurin has a shiny new contract after an offseason hold-in, and the front office has added key pieces to the team like OT Laremy Tunsil and WR Deebo Samuel. The defense will continue to improve, and if Daniels can continue to build on his stellar rookie campaign, the Commanders should be contenders for the next decade plus.

2024 · Midseason

Ranked #6 · 7-2

I will try to keep this unbiased, as a Commanders fan. Without question, the biggest surprise of the 2024 NFL season has been the early success of the Commanders. When new owner Josh Harris and newly appointed GM Adam Peters selected LSU QB Jayden Daniels with the second pick in the draft, they had no idea the efficiency, and stability he would provide to, over the last decade, one of the worst offenses in the league. All Daniels has done through his first nine games is dominate. The Commanders have turned the ball over three times through nine weeks. I'll let that statistic sink in. That's the best mark through nine weeks since 1933, and they're doing it with a rookie quarterback. With the recent addition of Saints All Pro CB Marshon Lattimore, this team is looking to go deep for the first time since 1991, when they went 15-1 and won the Super Bowl. First year Commanders Head Coach Dan Quinn has Washington fans enveloped in an all-encompassing euphoria, and the fast track to Coach of the Year.
